Five days on the Riviera, based in Nice, without a car.

One hotel. No car. Five days. And no, you do not need to squeeze Monaco, Cannes and Saint-Tropez in before Tuesday night.

Let us save you the first mistake: changing hotels every two days. Nice is not always the most spectacular base. We do not care. Look at the train map and it becomes horribly convincing.

The Mametas verdict

This is the plan we would give our own friends: five nights, sensible shoes and absolutely no good reason to spend the holiday inside a rental car.

How long have you got? 3 days · 5 days · 7 days. Same base, different amount of ambition.

Before you arrive

Sort the boring bits first. Flying into Nice? Choose tram, TER, coach or taxi from the airport → Already thinking about day trips? See the train, bus, tram and ticket guide →

Location before sea view

Stay near a tram and within a sensible walk of Nice-Ville. Old Nice has the charm. Jean Médecin and Dubouchage have the logistics. You cannot have everything on the same corner, pichoun. Pick your priority.

Do not rent a car for these five days

The coastal railway handles the long east-west moves in this itinerary. Buses and walking cover the places the train does not. A car becomes useful when you add inland villages, remote beaches or a Saint-Tropez plan with awkward seasonal connections.

Choose the mood, then the hotel

For this itinerary, central Nice is the practical answer. Fine. The interesting question is what you want to find when the day trips are over.

Five days: keep the full contrast.

Use the complete route below. It gives Nice enough time to feel like a base, then changes direction and mood rather than collecting neighbouring towns for the sake of it.

Day 1

Nice: learn the base before escaping it

Do not escape Nice at 9 am on your first morning. We can see you coming. Do Old Nice, the market and the sea. Climb if your legs agree. You do not need to finish Nice. You need to start understanding it.

Effort: Old Nice is flat but busy. Castle Hill is a staircase climb; a lift may run depending on opening times.

Do: leave one meal unplanned so the first day can absorb a late flight or an early collapse.

Next decision: understand Nice before choosing what to skip →

Day 2

Villefranche and Cap-Ferrat: the beautiful, slower day

Start in Villefranche, five minutes from Nice by train. Then choose: a walk, a garden or a long lunch. Not all three plus six coves. Cap-Ferrat is gorgeous. It is not a scavenger hunt.

The catch: gradients and walking time matter. Choose one main Cap-Ferrat objective rather than treating the peninsula as a checklist.

Choose your Villefranche / Cap-Ferrat version →

Day 3

Monaco and Menton: spectacle, then exhale

Monaco first, Menton after. Yes, the contrast is slightly violent. That is exactly why we like it. The train makes the pairing easy (about 20 minutes from Nice to Monaco, and Menton is on the same line), provided you do not turn Monaco into an inventory before lunch.

Effort: the Rock sits well above the port, but Monaco has 79 public lifts and 35 escalators. In Menton, the climb to Saint-Michel through the old town is the only real effort.

Do: check live rail information on the morning of travel. The route is frequent, but works and disruption do not consult your mood board.

Decide how much Monaco you actually want →

Day 4

Antibes: the western counterpoint

We really like Antibes, about 20 minutes west of Nice by train. There, we said it. Old town, ramparts, beach. It all fits. Add Cannes only if you actually want Cannes. Proximity is still not an instruction.

The catch: Cap d’Antibes is not the same thing as central Antibes. Reaching coves around the cape changes the transport calculation.

Day 5

Èze, or admit that a slow day is allowed

Èze is gorgeous. At 11 am in August, you will simply not be the only genius who had the idea. Take the bus to the hilltop village. If the crowds, heat or your legs say no, stay in Nice. Nobody is taking points away.

Effort: even after the bus, the village is steep, cobbled and full of steps. If stairs are a problem, choose the slow Nice day.

Mametas rule: the flexible day is not a failure of planning. It is the part that makes the rest feel like a holiday.

What not to miss on this itinerary

The essentials come down to five decisions: Nice; Villefranche plus one Cap-Ferrat choice; the Rock in Monaco then Menton old town; Old Antibes and the ramparts; finally Èze or a properly slow Nice day. Everything else is optional.

Getting around

The regional train is your spine. Antibes, Monaco, Menton: train first. Èze village and parts of the Cap: bus (line 82 goes up to Èze village from Nice). Check the timetable for the actual day.

Average TER times from Nice-Ville, according to SNCF: Villefranche-sur-Mer 5 minutes, Antibes and Monaco about 20, Menton about 40. A single ticket costs €3.60 to Villefranche, €7 to Antibes and €7.70 to Menton.

The SudAzur Explore Pass currently covers train, tram and bus travel in the Alpes-Maritimes and Monaco for 3, 7 or 14 days. Compare it with individual fares for your exact sequence; “pass” is not French for “automatically cheaper”.

Train, bus, taxi or car?

Train: default for Antibes, Cannes, Monaco and Menton. Bus: use it when geography leaves the railway behind; Èze village and parts of Cap-Ferrat are the obvious examples. Taxi / ride-hail: buy back time for awkward first/last kilometres or late returns. Car: worth the hassle only for inland villages, remote coves or a day whose value depends on several poorly connected stops.

Bad weather is not a cancelled trip

If Nice wakes up grey, switch rather than sulk: use the Nice-in-the-rain plan. Before adding another day trip, read 7 ways to get the Riviera wrong.

When the heat is brutal

Move climbs, exposed old towns and longer walks to the morning. Use the hottest hours for museums, lunch, shade or a swim, then resume later. On those days, Èze at midday is not a character-building exercise worth scheduling.

Book ahead: the short checklist

Reserve the things whose absence would really spoil the trip. For this route, that means the hotel first and only the timed experience you actually care about.

Day 7

Choose one extra direction

Cannes if you want the Croisette, hotel theatre and an easy coastal train; Saint-Paul-de-Vence if you want an inland contrast. One is enough.

The trade-off: seven days gives you breathing room. A second hotel is possible, but the Nice base still works; do not create a luggage problem just because the calendar got longer.
