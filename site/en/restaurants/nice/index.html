Where to eat in Nice

Twelve addresses. Some we love. A few we love for one very specific reason. That’s more useful than pretending every restaurant is a must.

MAMETAS SAYS

Tell us what you feel like eating and how much you want to spend. We’ll narrow it down. And please, no sad salad on the Promenade unless you genuinely wanted the salad.

If you want to decide in 20 seconds

Niçoise and simple: Chez Pipo (€), La Mérenda (€€), Chez Acchiardo (€€), Lou Balico (€€). Bistro / produce: Bistrot d’Antoine (€€), Olive & Artichaut (€€), Chez Davia (€€), Café des Musiciens (€€), Le Canon (€€). Fish / sea: Peixes (€€), Le Plongeoir (€€€). Big night: JAN (€€€€).

In Nice, a salade niçoise is a raw salad, hard-boiled egg aside: tomatoes, peppers, broad beans in season, tuna and anchovies, with no potatoes or green beans. The Cuisine Nissarde label in a window is the quickest sign of a kitchen that respects local recipes.

4 rue Raoul Bosio · Old Nice

La Mérenda

€€ · Niçoise cuisine · Tiny, no theatre

Best for: a serious local lunch or dinner.

We really like La Mérenda. Tiny room, serious Niçoise cooking, no circus. But: it fills up. Book.

13 rue Bavastro · Port

Chez Pipo

€ · Socca & Niçoise specialities · Popular

Best for: eating local without ceremony.

Come for the socca. That’s the point. Eat it hot and don’t overthink it. But: this is quick, casual and popular.

Source ↗Google Maps ↗
38 rue Droite · Old Nice

Chez Acchiardo

€€ · Niçoise cuisine · Family-run, traditional

Best for: a first proper local meal.

We like it for a first proper Niçoise meal. Family run, classic, no need to reinvent the wheel. But: if you want avant garde, keep walking.

Source ↗Google Maps ↗
27 rue de la Préfecture · Old Nice

Le Bistrot d’Antoine

€€ · French bistro · Lively

Best for: a central dinner without tourist folklore.

One of our favourites for a lively central dinner. But: it’s popular. Book. Optimism is not a reservation.

4 rue Jacques Médecin · Opéra

Peixes

€€ · Fish, raw dishes, Mediterranean · Contemporary

Best for: a break from traditional Niçoise food.

Ceviches, tartares, carpaccios and fish in a current Mediterranean register. But: more urban than romantic.

20-22 avenue Saint-Jean-Baptiste

Lou Balico

€€ · Niçoise cuisine · Popular institution

Best for: local classics and a proper appetite.

Socca, petits farcis and Niçoise staples. But: tradition. Good.

Source ↗Google Maps ↗
12 rue Lascaris · Port

JAN

€€€€ · Fine dining · Intimate

Best for: the big night.

Jan Hendrik van der Westhuizen combines French and South African influences in a very small dining room. But: price and booking effort match the occasion.

Source ↗Google Maps ↗
60 boulevard Franck Pilatte

Le Plongeoir

€€€ · Mediterranean · Spectacular sea view

Best for: lunch or dinner when the setting matters.

One of our favourite views in Nice. Yes, really. But: part of the bill is the setting, so come because you actually want the setting.

Source ↗Google Maps ↗
6 rue Sainte-Réparate · Old Nice

Olive & Artichaut

€€ · Regional cuisine · Small bistro

Best for: local produce with more precision.

The restaurant works with regional produce and is listed by Michelin at €€. But: small house, so book.

Source ↗Google Maps ↗
11 bis rue Grimaldi · Centre

Chez Davia

€€ · Niçoise cuisine · Warm, family-run

Best for: tradition without postcard staging.

We like Chez Davia precisely because it feels like a neighbourhood table. Niçoise cooking, fresh produce, terrace. But: don’t come looking for spectacle. That’s the compliment.

Source ↗Google Maps ↗
13 avenue Auber · Musiciens

Café des Musiciens

€€ · Market cooking · Small, calm

Best for: produce-led lunch or a discreet dinner.

The menu follows seasons and local producers; the restaurant currently lists a lunch menu around €30. But: few seats and limited opening days.

Source ↗Google Maps ↗
23 rue Meyerbeer · Carré d’Or

Le Canon

€€ · Farm-to-table / market · Wine-bistro mood

Best for: local produce and natural wine.

Michelin lists it at €€ and highlights locally sourced, often organic produce and natural wines. But: less brochure Riviera, more insiders’ bistro.
