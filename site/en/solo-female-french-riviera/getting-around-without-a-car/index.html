Getting around the French Riviera alone - without a car

For the coastal Riviera, public transport is not the compromise. It is usually the smarter system. The trick is knowing which trips are genuinely effortless and which ones become a bus timetable hobby.

THE MAMETAS TAKE

Do not rent a car for a first solo Riviera trip built around Nice. Use rail for the coast, tram for Nice and a taxi/ride-hail only when the last kilometre becomes silly.

Landing alone: Nice Airport is unusually easy

Nice Airport says tram line 2 links Terminals 1 and 2 with Jean Médecin and Port Lympia in under 30 minutes, with weekday trams roughly every 7 to 8 minutes. That removes one of the most annoying first-day decisions.

The solo rule for evenings

Do not memorise a “last train” from a blog. Timetables move. Check the actual day in SNCF Connect/TER and the Lignes d’Azur evening network. The Nice tram runs late, but the precise last departure depends on line, direction, day and season.

Day trips ranked by friction

  • Very easy: Villefranche, Antibes, Cannes, Monaco, Menton.
  • Easy but with an extra layer: Èze village, because the rail station is on the coast and the village is uphill; plan the bus connection.
  • Beautiful but less efficient: Saint-Paul-de-Vence, Cap-Ferrat loops and some hill villages, where bus timing matters more.
  • Do not force it: Saint-Tropez as a casual rail day trip. Go deliberately.

Small habits that save stress

  • Buy/validate tickets before boarding where required.
  • Keep the hotel pin saved offline.
  • On a late return, sit near other passengers rather than at the empty end of a train.
  • If a disruption leaves you stranded, solve the night first; argue with customer service tomorrow.

DINNER OUT OF TOWN?

Check the last return before you order dessert.

Regional trains are excellent for daytime Riviera trips, but evening frequency thins out and the last useful return depends on the destination, day and season. For Monaco, Menton, Antibes or Cannes, open SNCF Connect on the day and check the final two departures back to Nice before dinner. If the last train would make the evening stressful, eat in Nice or budget for a taxi/VTC home.

Nice itself is easier late: the tram runs substantially later than most day-trip trains, but published times still change by day and works. Treat the app/operator timetable as the source of truth.
