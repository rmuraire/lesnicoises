Lérins Islands: Sainte-Marguerite or Saint-Honorat?

Cannes fifteen minutes behind you, pine trees, coves and no reason to optimise the day. The only decision that matters is choosing the right island.

MAMETAS SAYS

First time, swimming, forest and the most freedom: Sainte-Marguerite. Monastery, vineyards, quiet and a more singular atmosphere: Saint-Honorat. Do one properly.

Sainte-Marguerite: the easy first choice

The larger island is about 3 km long, with pine and eucalyptus forest, coves, the Fort Royal / Iron Mask museum and 22 km of paths managed through the protected natural area. It gives you more room to walk away from the landing stage and find your own rhythm.

Choose it for: swimming, walking, picnic, family day, Fort Royal, or simply a nature reset from Cannes.

Boat. Trans Côte d’Azur, Riviera Lines and Horizon serve Sainte-Marguerite from Cannes. Trans Côte d’Azur says individual travellers do not need a reservation; online tickets remain useful to remove ticket-office admin. Official ferry overview ↗

Saint-Honorat: the quieter decision

Saint-Honorat is smaller, around 1.5 km long, and organised around the Lérins Abbey, vineyards and monastic landscape. Planaria, operated by the monks, runs the crossing from Quai Laubeuf in Cannes.

The fortified monastery can be visited free of charge. The island has public toilets and water fountains; one restaurant and one snack bar operate near the landing stage and are closed from January to mid-February according to Cannes Tourism.

Boat. Planaria currently lists the online adult return fare at €19.50. Tickets and live times ↗

Entre nous

On Saint-Honorat, around twenty-five monks still tend eight hectares of vines; the island has grown wine since the Middle Ages. If you pick the quiet island, leave room in your bag: the abbey shop sells its wines and liqueurs, a better souvenir than anything on the Croisette.

the Mametas

Can you do both in one day?

Technically, with enough timetable management. Practically, Mametas says no. Cannes Tourism confirms there is no direct shuttle between the two islands; you must return to the embarkation system and change operator. Two islands in one day gives you more transport and less island.

Swimming, food and what to pack

Sainte-Marguerite has multiple coves and wild swimming spots. Saint-Honorat also works for a swim, but its identity is calmer and more monastic. Both islands have picnic areas. Bring water anyway, sun protection, shoes that tolerate paths and something to swim in.

Fire is strictly prohibited and both islands are non-smoking areas. On Saint-Honorat, dress appropriately around the monastery. These are islands.

How long?

Half-day: possible for Saint-Honorat or a focused Sainte-Marguerite visit. Full day: better if swimming is part of the plan. If you catch yourself calculating how to leave after ninety minutes so you can also fit Antibes, Cannes and a rooftop cocktail into the afternoon, the island has already lost.

Booking: Sainte-Marguerite does not require advance reservation for individual passengers with Trans Côte d’Azur. Saint-Honorat tickets can be bought online. For fixed summer dates, buying ahead is still the less annoying option.
