DAY TRIPS • GRASSE

Grasse: worth the trip for perfume?

Yes, if perfume is the reason you go. Grasse is not another seaside stop: it is the hill town where the perfume trade learned its craft. Go to make a fragrance and understand how one is made, not for one more pretty old town.

Go if you want to make a perfume or see how one is made. Skip it if you only want another hilltop old town: the coast has several, closer to the train. From Cannes or Antibes, Grasse is an easy half-day. From Nice, give it the whole day.

Who should go, and who should not

Go if perfume, design, luxury or craft interest you; if you are travelling with a teenager or a student who wants to make something rather than visit something; or if you need a strong indoor plan for a rainy day.

Skip it on a three-day first trip built around Nice, Monaco and Èze. Grasse sits inland, at the western end of the rail line: from Nice, the round trip costs more time than the workshop itself.

Make your own perfume: which workshop?

This is the reason to come. Three historic houses run workshops for individual visitors, and all three should be booked ahead.

Without a car, choose Molinard. It is in town, lasts an hour, runs sessions at 11:00, 14:00 and 16:00 in English and French, and leaves time for the museum. For the most complete session, choose Galimard: longer, a 100 ml bottle, from age 8, in English, French, German or Italian, closed on Sundays. It is outside the old town: the tourist office lists a bus stop within 500 m, so plan the bus or a taxi.

Free, and worth an hour: Fragonard’s historic factory

At 20 boulevard Fragonard, the historic factory offers a free guided visit, and its small perfume museum on the third floor is free too. Open every day from 9:00; closing time varies with the season. Yes, the visit ends in the shop. That is the business model, not a trap. Large groups of more than 15 must book.

The museum: Musée International de la Parfumerie

The serious counterpart to the factory tours: the history of perfume, its raw materials and its bottles, at 2 boulevard du Jeu de Ballon. Open every day, 10:00 to 18:00, and until 19:00 in July and August. It closes on the first Monday of January, February, March, October, November and December.

The ticket is €10 (€7.50 reduced) and also covers the Bonnard Museum in Le Cannet within four days. Entry is free for under-18s, and for everyone on the first Sunday of the month from October to April. The museum’s gardens are in Mouans-Sartoux, a separate trip.

Getting there without a car

Direct TER trains run from Nice-Ville to Grasse in about an hour, through Antibes and Cannes. The catch is at the other end: Mèfi, Grasse station sits at the foot of the hill, well below the old town. Take a local bus up to the bus station (gare routière), a few steps from the old town, rather than climbing with a bag.

In our travel-time study, a return trip to Grasse took, door to door: 2 h 31 from Cannes, 2 h 55 from Antibes, 4 h 21 from Nice and 5 h 16 from Menton. If perfume is central to your trip, a western base makes Grasse a half-day rather than an expedition. Cannes as a base →

How long, and when?

Half a day from Cannes or Antibes: one workshop and the Fragonard factory. A full day from Nice: workshop, museum and lunch. Where to eat in Grasse →

The workshops and museums run all year. The flowers do not: May is rose season, late summer is jasmine. Grasse plus Saint-Paul plus Èze in one day is not a plan. It is a bus timetable.

Booking: book the workshop before you take the train. Sessions run at set times and the good slots go first in summer. Check the museum’s first-Monday closures in the off-season.
