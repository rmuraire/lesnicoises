First: Èze Village and Èze-sur-Mer are not the same place

This is the classic confusion, and it wastes more time than most bad restaurant recommendations. Èze Village is the medieval hilltop village, 429 metres above the sea, with the Exotic Garden and the views. Èze-sur-Mer is down on the coast, with the railway station and the start of the Nietzsche Trail. Different stop, different day, and definitely not a casual uphill transfer with luggage.

THE MAMETAS VERDICT

Èze earns a place on a first five- or seven-day Riviera trip. With three days, it becomes a choice: go because the hilltop village genuinely matters to you.

From Nice without a car: the easy version

Nice Côte d’Azur Tourist Office lists buses 82 and 602 from Nice to the village. From Beaulieu-sur-Mer and Èze-sur-Mer station, bus 83 reaches the village. If the village is the goal, this is the clean version. Cardio is optional.

What about the Nietzsche Trail?

The trail links Èze-sur-Mer with the hilltop village. The train drops you at the foot of the path. Expect a real climb with steps, earth and paved sections. Excellent if you deliberately want a walk; less charming if you thought you had discovered a clever shortcut.

What actually deserves your time

The Exotic Garden. It sits at the top of the village and is the natural high point of the visit. The tourist office gives an average visit time of about an hour. Come for the views as much as the plants.

The village itself. The lanes are small. That is rather the point. Walk, look, then resist turning every doorway into a content strategy.

One proper pause. Coffee, lunch or twenty minutes with no optimisation whatsoever. Èze becomes exhausting mainly when treated as a military operation.

The Nietzsche Trail only if you want the walk. You do not need to climb it to “do Èze”. Philosophy remains optional too.

What is the best time to visit?

Our editorial preference is early morning or later afternoon. The village is small, so crowds become physical very quickly. At peak hours in high season, there is a real risk of studying other people’s shoulders instead of the Mediterranean.

If the Exotic Garden matters, check the day’s hours: closing times change through the year. In September 2026 it is listed until 19:30, with progressively earlier closing through autumn and winter.

Do you need to book?

Book the things that would genuinely spoil the day if unavailable: a destination hotel, a specific restaurant, or any timed experience you care about. Ordinary transport should stay flexible; check the current timetable rather than freezing the whole trip around an old screenshot.

Should you stay overnight in Èze?

For a first Riviera trip, usually not. Nice or Villefranche are stronger bases if you want to explore widely. Èze becomes a good overnight decision when the place itself is the experience: dinner, hotel, the village after day visitors leave and the early morning before they come back.

La Chèvre d’Or is exactly that sort of choice. Nobody books it to optimise TER connections.

Èze or Saint-Paul-de-Vence?

Choose Èze for the Mediterranean panorama and visual drama. Choose Saint-Paul-de-Vence for modern art, Fondation Maeght and a deeper inland day. They are both hilltop villages; calling them interchangeable is lazy. Doing both in three days can be lazy in the opposite direction.

When we would skip it

You have only three days and still have not given Nice a proper day.

Mobility is severely limited: the village is steep and cobbled, and the Exotic Garden is listed as not wheelchair-accessible.

You were planning Èze, Monaco, Menton and dinner in Cannes on the same day. No. Even the Mametas have limits.
