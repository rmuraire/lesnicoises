Grasse: the perfume town, best as a day trip

Grasse is not a seaside town and does not pretend to be one. It is a working hill town where perfume became a craft, then an industry. Come for the day, make a fragrance, walk the old town, then sleep on the coast.

Why Grasse is different

Most Riviera day trips sell a view. Grasse sells a skill. In 2018, UNESCO inscribed the perfume know-how of the Pays de Grasse on its list of intangible cultural heritage: growing perfume plants, knowing and transforming natural raw materials, and the art of composing a fragrance. You do not come here for the coastline. You come to understand what is inside the bottle, and to make one yourself.

THE MAMETAS VERDICT

Grasse earns a day if perfume interests you even slightly. If it does not, the coast has prettier hilltop villages closer to the train. Either way, it is a day trip, not a base.

Getting there without a car

Direct TER trains run to Grasse along the coastal line from Nice, Antibes and Cannes. The catch is at the other end: Mèfi, the station is at the foot of the hill, well below the old town. Bus 5 to the town centre, among other lines, leaves from opposite the station. Take it rather than climbing with a bag.

What actually deserves your time

A perfume workshop. The reason to come. Molinard, Galimard and Fragonard each run sessions where you compose your own fragrance in about one to two hours. They differ in length, location and what you take home. Which workshop to book →

Fragonard’s historic factory. A free guided visit and a small free perfume museum, open every day. The visit ends in the shop. That is the business model, not a trap.

The Musée International de la Parfumerie. The serious version: history, raw materials, bottles. €10, a ticket that also covers the Bonnard Museum in Le Cannet, and free for under-18s.

Notre-Dame-du-Puy cathedral. Free, and easy to miss if you only follow the shops. Inside: works attributed to Rubens or his school, and The Washing of the Feet (1754), one of the rare religious paintings by Fragonard, who was born in Grasse. Closed to visitors on Sundays.

The old town itself. Steep lanes, washed façades, very little theatre. It is a town people live and work in, not a set.

When to go

The workshops and museums run all year. The flowers do not. Grasse celebrates the rose in May with Expo Rose (8 to 10 May in 2026) and jasmine at the turn of August with the Fête du Jasmin (31 July to 2 August in 2026). Check the next dates before you plan around them. Outside those weeks, come for the craft rather than the fields.

Do you need to book?

Book the workshop. Sessions run at set times and the good slots go first in summer. The Fragonard factory, the cathedral and the museum need no booking for individual visitors. Trains stay flexible: check the day’s timetable rather than an old screenshot.

Should you stay overnight in Grasse?

For a first Riviera trip, no. Grasse is inland and at the western end of the line: as a base, it would turn every coastal day into an excursion. Our hotel selection does not include Grasse for that reason. If perfume matters, sleep in Cannes or Antibes and make Grasse a half-day.

Grasse or Saint-Paul-de-Vence?

Choose Grasse for perfume, a working town and a day with your hands busy. Choose Saint-Paul-de-Vence for modern art, Fondation Maeght and a pretty walled village. Both are inland. On a short trip, pick one: doing both turns the holiday into a bus timetable.

When we would skip it

You have three days based in Nice and still have not given Nice a proper day.

Perfume leaves you cold. Grasse without a workshop is a pleasant old town, not a reason to spend half a day on trains.

It is Sunday and the Galimard workshop or the cathedral is the point: both are closed to visitors that day.

You were planning Grasse, Saint-Paul and Èze on the same day. No. Even the Mametas have limits.
