Cannes: the polished base that actually works

Yes, the Croisette is the obvious bit. The more useful story is that Cannes is compact, sandy, walkable and well connected by rail. Glamour is optional. Good logistics are not.

Is Cannes worth visiting?

Yes. Just do not confuse “worth visiting” with “must spend three days inspecting luxury shop windows”. Cannes is at its best when you use the contrast properly: market and old town in the morning, polished seafront later, sand when you want it, and the Lérins Islands when you want the city to disappear entirely.

THE MAMETAS VERDICT

For a first Riviera trip, Cannes is an excellent day trip and a credible base. Stay here if sandy beaches, a compact centre and a hotel-led holiday matter more than having the Riviera’s widest choice of neighbourhoods and nightlife. If transport flexibility is the priority, Nice still wins the spreadsheet. Cannes wins more often when the spreadsheet has been put away.

Should you stay in Cannes or visit for the day?

Stay in Cannes if: you want to walk from hotel to beach to dinner, you like the idea of a polished seafront, or the hotel itself is part of the experience.

Visit from Nice if: you want the broader restaurant and city life of Nice, plan several eastern Riviera days, or simply want one clean western excursion without moving luggage.

Do not choose Cannes just because it looks expensive. It can be. It can also be remarkably simple: station, market, old town and public beach all sit within a very manageable day.

One day in Cannes, in the right order

Morning: Forville first, then Le Suquet

Start at Marché Forville while the day still feels local. Then climb into Le Suquet and the old town. The official tourism map puts the market, historic quarter, viewpoints and Old Port into the same natural sequence. This is the Cannes that existed before red carpets learned to reproduce.

Late morning: walk the Croisette once

Walk enough of the Croisette to understand it. There is no prize for completing every metre. The Palais des Festivals, grand hotels and seafront are part of the city’s identity; treat them as a chapter.

Afternoon: choose beach or islands

If you want an easy day, take the sand. Cannes has 18 public beaches and more than seven kilometres of sandy coastline according to the tourist office. If you want the stronger excursion, take the boat to the Lérins Islands. Trying to do both properly in one afternoon is how leisure turns into project management.

What actually deserves your time

Le Suquet + Old Port. The historic quarter is the useful antidote to the idea that Cannes is only a hotel façade.

Forville Market. Go in the morning. It gives the centre texture and makes lunch decisions considerably easier.

The Croisette. Yes, obviously. Walk it, look at it, then allow yourself to leave it.

One proper sandy beach. This is Cannes’ practical advantage over Nice, where pebbles remain impressively committed to the brand.

Lérins Islands. If you have the time, this is the excursion that changes the mood rather than merely changing the postcode.

Cannes without a car

This is one of the city’s strongest arguments. The main station is central, and regional TER trains connect Cannes with Antibes, Cagnes-sur-Mer, Nice, Villefranche-sur-Mer and the eastern Riviera. For a first trip, a car is not a requirement; for many itineraries it is additional luggage with insurance.

If you stay in Cannes, use the train for Antibes and Nice. If you stay in Nice, use the same logic in reverse. Save the car for the days where the railway genuinely stops helping. For the trip-level decision, return to Plan; for tickets and fares, open Practical.

When Cannes is the wrong choice

You have only three days and are already giving one of them to Antibes. The west does not need two auditions.

Your dream trip is mostly Villefranche, Monaco and Menton. Cannes is then the wrong end of the line to call “convenient”.

You are visiting during a major congress or event and the hotel rates have decided to become performance art. Check the calendar before assuming an ordinary week.

Cannes or Nice?

Choose Nice for transport reach, neighbourhood variety, nightlife and the easiest all-round first base. Choose Cannes for sand, a smaller walkable centre, polished hotels and a trip that feels more resort than city. Neither is objectively “better”. One is simply better for the holiday you are actually taking.
