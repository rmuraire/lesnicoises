PLACES · Antibes

Antibes: should you stay here?

An old town, a sandy beach, a useful station and Cannes within easy reach. Antibes is the Riviera for people who enjoy several good ideas fitting into the same plan.

Antibes makes a less theatrical promise than Cannes and a less obvious logistical one than Nice. That is exactly why it works: old town, beach, local life and rail access sit together without turning every day into a project-management exercise.

The Mametas verdict

If Nice feels too urban and Cannes too staged, Antibes is probably your answer. For a first trip without a car, it is our strongest alternative to Nice.

Antibes as a base

The old town is walkable, the station is close enough to make day trips realistic and the sea does not require an expedition. You can spend one full day without transport, then take the train to Nice or Cannes the next morning.

Evenings feel more local and less polished than Cannes. That is a feature if you want a seaside town rather than an hotel showroom.

Beaches: a genuine advantage

Antibes has sandy beaches accessible from town, which changes the rhythm of a stay. La Gravette is particularly easy to combine with an old-town day. Juan-les-Pins adds another beach-led mood, with more resort energy and nightlife.

Do not automatically combine Antibes, Juan-les-Pins and the entire Cap into one day because the map has placed them politely next to each other. Geography occasionally has a poor sense of humour.

Cap d’Antibes: beautiful, less immediate

The Cap is a destination in itself: hotels, coves, villas and walks. It complements Antibes perfectly but does not behave like its centre. Stay on the Cap and you choose more calm and hotel time; stay in Antibes and you choose walking, old-town life and the station.

Mametas rule

Antibes and the Cap are neighbours.

Choose the rhythm first. A taxi between two beautiful places remains a taxi.

Transport: excellent for the west, still easy towards Nice

Antibes station makes Nice and Cannes simple. For a trip focused on the western Riviera, the logic is strong. Monaco and Menton remain possible, but Nice becomes the more central base if eastern days dominate the plan.

A car becomes useful when the itinerary shifts towards isolated beaches, inland villages or harder-to-reach Cap addresses. In central Antibes it can mostly become something expensive you pay to park.

Where to stay in Antibes

The useful choice here is geographical: central Antibes for old town, restaurants, station and beach on foot; the Cap when the hotel, calm and sea should take a bigger role.

The right hotel depends on your budget and how you travel.

Antibes or Cannes?

Choose Cannes for palaces, the Croisette, private beaches and a more concentrated glamorous stay. Choose Antibes for a stronger old town, a less performative atmosphere and a better balance between sightseeing, beach and everyday life.

For a first four- or five-day trip, we would choose Antibes before Cannes if Nice is off the table. Cannes will survive this further humiliation.

What not to miss in Antibes

Antibes is at its best when you keep the day compact: old town, art, sea. The Cap is the optional second act.

Old Antibes + Marché Provençal + ramparts

Treat them as one walk. This is the core of the town.

Picasso Museum

The obvious cultural stop and one of the few museums on the coast that fits naturally into a beach-town day.

Plage de la Gravette

Sand immediately beside the old town. Use the convenience instead of commuting to a prettier beach for the sake of it.

Cap d’Antibes

Add it only when you have the time for coves and the coastal path. It is another rhythm.

Three practical decisions

Train for Old Antibes

From Nice or Cannes, rail drops you into a visit that works naturally on foot.

Cap d’Antibes is another rhythm

Coves and coastal paths need more time and transport.

Rain: culture before stubbornness

Picasso Museum and a long lunch beat pretending a beach day is still happening.
