Where to stay on the French Riviera without a car

A car on the Riviera looks excellent in advertising. In an underground car park in July, behind the empégué who has been looking for his ticket for six minutes, considerably less so. Mèfi: watch out, pichoun.

This page answers one question: where to stay without a car. To decide whether you need to rent one, use Plan. For fares, tickets and train/tram/bus mechanics, use Practical.

For a car-free trip, Nice is the simplest base. Station, tram, buses and coastal trains: dégun is giving you a medal for renting a car just to spend the holiday between two SUVs. Your suitcase has wheels. Use them.

What Mametas means by “Central Nice”.
It is not an official neighbourhood. For hotel hunting, think of the useful corridor from Nice-Ville / Gare Thiers down Avenue Jean Médecin to Place Masséna, plus the pedestrian zone and Carré d’Or towards the seafront. That puts the main station, tram, shopping streets, restaurants, Old Nice and the Promenade within a very manageable walking radius.
NICE-VILLE → AVENUE JEAN MÉDECIN → PLACE MASSÉNA

Central Nice

See the practical Nice shortlist →

The most practical choice for a first trip. Walk to the sea, station and tram. Not every corner is romantic. That is exactly why it works.

Avenue Jean Médecin is the useful spine: Nice-Ville sits at the northern end of the central visitor zone, Place Masséna anchors the southern end, and the pedestrian streets of the Carré d’Or spread west towards the Promenade. The tourist office itself describes Masséna as the crossroads between Old Nice, Avenue Jean Médecin and the Promenade.

Why it works without a car: tram line 1 runs along the Jean Médecin / Masséna axis, while line 2 meets the centre at Jean Médecin and continues directly to the airport and Port Lympia. Nice-Ville to Place Masséna is about 1.3 km, roughly a 15-minute walk.

COURS SALEYA • PLACE ROSSETTI • OLD TOWN

Old Nice

See the lively Nice shortlist →

More charm, more activity, less silence. Excellent if you want to go out on foot. Less excellent if your sleep requires an entire city to go to bed at 10 pm because you booked inside Old Nice. Hu. Old Nice did not receive the memo.

This is the dense historic quarter around Cours Saleya, Place Rossetti and the narrow lanes behind the seafront. Restaurants, bars, markets and small shops are concentrated in a very small area, much of it pedestrian. The sea is only a few minutes away on foot.

The trade-off: atmosphere is excellent, suitcase logistics are less elegant. If your hotel is deep inside the old lanes, check the exact walking route from the tram before booking.

Useful stops. Tram T1 · Opéra-Vieille Ville or Cathédrale-Vieille Ville. Google Maps ↗

PLACE GARIBALDI → PORT LYMPIA → FRANCK PILATTE

The Port

Compare the Nice hotel shortlist →

A good compromise for eastern Nice, the Cap de Nice swimming spots and easy access back to the centre.

Think Place Garibaldi, rue Bonaparte and Port Lympia. Old Nice is close, the Port itself is walkable, and the coastline towards La Réserve and Coco Beach starts from here.

Why it works: tram line 2 terminates at Port Lympia and runs directly through Jean Médecin to the airport. Why it is not Central Nice: Nice-Ville station is less convenient if you plan to make a TER day trip every morning. Choose the Port because you want the Port.

5 MINUTES FROM NICE BY TER

Villefranche-sur-Mer

See the short Villefranche hotel selection →

Smaller, very beautiful and connected by coastal train. A strong choice if you accept fewer services in exchange for a setting Nice itself can envy.

SNCF currently lists an average journey of about 5 minutes between Nice and Villefranche-sur-Mer, with around 60 round trips a day. That makes Villefranche much less isolated than it looks on the map.

The catch: Villefranche climbs. Five minutes on the train does not mean five flat minutes with luggage. Before booking, look at the hotel’s actual walking route from the station.

CANNES STATION • CROISETTE • PALAIS

Central Cannes

See Cannes hotels by trip style →

If your days point west, Cannes becomes a very sensible car-free base. Stay close to the station and Croisette and most of the useful centre is on foot.

Cannes station sits in the heart of town, only a few hundred metres from the Palais des Festivals and the Croisette. SNCF currently lists Cannes-Antibes at about 10 minutes on average and Nice-Cannes at about 35 minutes.

Choose Cannes over Nice if your trip is mainly Cannes, Antibes, Mougins or the western Riviera. If Monaco, Menton and the eastern coast dominate the plan, Nice is the more balanced railway hub.

MAMETAS SAYS

First Riviera trip with several day trips: Central Nice. Nightlife and atmosphere on the doorstep: Old Nice. Port, sea and airport convenience: the Port. Beauty first, fewer services: Villefranche. Western Riviera-heavy trip: Cannes.

The principle is simple: if you plan to visit several coastal towns, check the real walking route to a TER station before anything else. The rooftop pool is lovely. It will not take you to Menton.
