WITH KIDS · FRENCH RIVIERA

With kids, the Riviera comes down to choosing the right base.

Sand or pebbles, a station within walking distance, a hotel where everyone sleeps: settle that first. The rest fits into one outing a day.

Mametas CheckedSources and practical details checked · 12 October 2026
Adobe Stock

Antibes if you want sand and everything on foot. Cannes for long sandy beaches and the big hotels’ kids’ clubs. Nice for free outings and trains everywhere, on pebble beaches. Menton for toddlers. Everywhere, regional trains are free under 4 and half price from 4 to 11.

Which base with kids

AntibesSandy beaches with a lifeguard post (Ponteil, Gravette), a compact old town, Nice 20 minutes away by train. The easiest base for a first trip.
CannesSeven kilometres of sand, a playground across from the Plage du Midi, boats to the Lérins Islands. Avoid May, festival month.
NiceFree parks and playgrounds, city museums free under 18, trains to Antibes and Monaco. The beaches are pebbles.
MentonLes Sablettes: very fine gravel on a gentle slope, with a play area. Quiet, about ten minutes from Monaco by train.
THE SIMPLE RULE

Nice means pebbles. It is still the best base for outings and trains; for a sand-digging day with little ones, Antibes is 20 minutes away by regional train.

About 700 m of public beach with lifeguards in high season, and the Square Mistral playground just across the road.

MENTON

Les Sablettes

Very fine gravel, a gentle slope and some of the shallowest water on the coast. Play area, showers, underground parking.

In Cannes, the Tétéoù scheme (mid-June to end of September) gives children a wristband so they can be found if they wander off. In Nice, lifeguard posts run from 1 July to mid-September depending on the beach, and ropes help you climb back up the pebbles. Flags and rules: swimming on the French Riviera.

Buses and trams in NiceFree travel under 11 is for residents of the Métropole only. The tram is step-free, which helps with a pushchair.

Heat, pharmacies, gear

  • In summer, go out early, stay somewhere cool through the hottest hours and offer drinks often: that is Santé publique France’s advice for children.
  • Pharmacie Riviera, 66 avenue Jean-Médecin in Nice, is open day and night (Sundays 8am–7pm). Elsewhere, 3237 gives the on-duty pharmacy; in an emergency, call 15 or 112.
  • Pushchair, travel cot, car seat: Yapuk and Co in Nice rents baby gear and delivers it to where you are staying.

Family FAQ

What is the best French Riviera town with kids?

Antibes for a first trip: sandy beaches with lifeguard posts, a compact old town and Nice 20 minutes away by train. Menton suits toddlers well, thanks to Les Sablettes beach.

Are Nice beaches good for children?

They are pebble beaches, with lifeguards in summer and ropes to help you climb back up. For playing in the sand, Antibes, Cannes or Les Sablettes in Menton work better.

How much is the train for a child?

On the region’s TER trains, it is free under 4 and half price from 4 to 11. The ZOU! Mini-Groupe ticket takes 30 to 50% off for 3 to 9 people travelling together.

Is Marineland Antibes open?

No, Marineland has been closed since January 2025. For a sea-themed day out, the Oceanographic Museum in Monaco is the easiest alternative (free under 4).

Which hotels have a kids’ club?

Among our picks: the Majestic (free) and the Martinez in Cannes, the Fairmont Monte Carlo in July and August (ages 4–12, free) and Le Domaine du Mas de Pierre during French school holidays (ages 3–12).
