Where to eat in Nice

Twelve addresses. At eight, we could tell you where to go. At twelve, we can finally help you choose.

MAMETAS SAYS

Start with what you feel like eating, then look at the price. Reversing that order has produced many sad salads on the Promenade.

HOW TO READ THE PRICES

€ easy on the wallet · €€ mid-range · €€€ expensive / occasion · €€€€ big night. These are relative positioning markers, not a promised bill: menus, seasons and appetites move.

If you want to decide in 20 seconds

Niçoise and simple: Chez Pipo (€), La Mérenda (€€), Chez Acchiardo (€€), Lou Balico (€€). Bistro / produce: Bistrot d’Antoine (€€), Olive & Artichaut (€€), Chez Davia (€€), Café des Musiciens (€€), Le Canon (€€). Fish / sea: Peixes (€€), Le Plongeoir (€€€). Big night: JAN (€€€€).

4 rue Raoul Bosio · Old Nice

La Mérenda

€€ · Niçoise cuisine · Tiny, no theatre · Best for: a serious local lunch or dinner.

Dominique Le Stanc’s institution stays focused on the Niçoise repertoire. The catch: small room and limited availability; plan ahead.

Source
13 rue Bavastro · Port

Chez Pipo

€ · Socca & Niçoise specialities · Popular · Best for: eating local without ceremony.

Socca is the main reason to come. The tourist office quotes an average spend around €15–20 in 2026. The catch: this is casual and local, not a three-hour dinner.

Source
38 rue Droite · Old Nice

Chez Acchiardo

€€ · Niçoise cuisine · Family-run, traditional · Best for: a first proper local meal.

A family house since 1927, carrying the Cuisine Nissarde label. The catch: classic by design; if you want avant-garde, keep walking.

Source
27 rue de la Préfecture · Old Nice

Le Bistrot d’Antoine

€€ · French bistro · Lively · Best for: a central dinner without tourist folklore.

A recognised bistro with southern accents. The catch: popular and busy; booking is more effective than optimism.

Michelin Guide
4 rue Jacques Médecin · Opéra

Peixes

€€ · Fish, raw dishes, Mediterranean · Contemporary · Best for: a break from traditional Niçoise food.

Ceviches, tartares, carpaccios and fish in a current Mediterranean register. The catch: more urban than romantic.

Source
20–22 avenue Saint-Jean-Baptiste

Lou Balico

€€ · Niçoise cuisine · Popular institution · Best for: local classics and a proper appetite.

Socca, petits farcis and Niçoise staples. The catch: tradition, not Scandinavian minimalism. Good.

Source
12 rue Lascaris · Port

JAN

€€€€ · Fine dining · Intimate · Best for: the big night.

Jan Hendrik van der Westhuizen combines French and South African influences in a very small dining room. The catch: price and booking effort match the occasion.

Source
60 boulevard Franck Pilatte

Le Plongeoir

€€€ · Mediterranean · Spectacular sea view · Best for: lunch or dinner when the setting matters.

Seasonal cooking above the sea. The catch: part of the bill is the setting; come because you actually want the setting.

Source
6 rue Sainte-Réparate · Old Nice

Olive & Artichaut

€€ · Regional cuisine · Small bistro · Best for: local produce with more precision.

The restaurant works with regional produce and is listed by Michelin at €€. The catch: small house, so book.

Source
11 bis rue Grimaldi · Centre

Chez Davia

€€ · Niçoise cuisine · Warm, family-run · Best for: tradition without postcard staging.

A family restaurant since 1953, with Niçoise cooking, fresh produce and a terrace. The catch: more neighbourhood table than spectacular destination — often a compliment.

Source
13 avenue Auber · Musiciens

Café des Musiciens

€€ · Market cooking · Small, calm · Best for: produce-led lunch or a discreet dinner.

The menu follows seasons and local producers; the restaurant currently lists a lunch menu around €30. The catch: few seats and limited opening days.

Source
23 rue Meyerbeer · Carré d’Or

Le Canon

€€ · Farm-to-table / market · Wine-bistro mood · Best for: local produce and natural wine.

Michelin lists it at €€ and highlights locally sourced, often organic produce and natural wines. The catch: less brochure Riviera, more insiders’ bistro.
