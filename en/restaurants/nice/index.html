Where to eat in Nice: 8 addresses worth your appetite

Nice has hundreds of restaurants. Wonderful. That also means hundreds of ways to eat badly, pichoun. These are eight places we would start with before handing dinner over to the first waiter waving a menu in the street.

MAMETAS SAYS

Mèfi: a full terrace is not a culinary qualification. In August, it mostly proves that a group of people got hungry at the same time.

4 rue Raoul Bosio, Vieux-Nice

La Mérenda

For Niçoise cooking without choreography around the plate.

Dominique Le Stanc runs this tiny 24-seat bistro devoted to Niçoise cooking. The tourist office lists two toques and a 2026 Bib Gourmand. The room is small and the message is fairly clear: you are here to eat.

Open Tuesday to Friday according to 2026 information. Booking is essential in our view, however touching your optimism may be.

Official source
13 rue Bavastro, Port

Chez Pipo

For wood-fired socca. It never asked to become a concept.

A socca institution approaching a century. The tourist office notes that locals and visitors have been coming for generations. Eat it hot, peppered and with your fingers. Do not ask for cutlery to look sophisticated.

In 2026, open Wednesday to Sunday, with Tuesdays added in July and August. The tourist office quotes an average spend around €15 to €20.

Official source
38 rue Droite, Vieux-Nice

Chez Acchiardo

For four generations of Niçoise cooking. You can survive a recipe that was not invented last Tuesday.

The Acchiardo family has run the place since 1927. It carries the Cuisine Nissarde label and remains one of Old Nice’s clearest reference points for local recipes.

38 rue Droite. Check the day’s opening times before going, even if the internet promised everything was simple.

Official source
27 rue de la Préfecture, Vieux-Nice

Le Bistrot d’Antoine

For bistro cooking, a blackboard and less folklore.

A lively bistro on rue de la Préfecture, listed as a Table Gourmande by Gault&Millau. Worked bistro cooking with southern accents, without any requirement to recreate Nice in 1890.

Open Tuesday to Saturday according to the guide. Booking remains a better strategy than staring at a full room looking offended.

Official source
4 rue Jacques Médecin, Opéra

Peixes

For contemporary fish once your socca obligations are fulfilled.

Fish and seafood, plenty of raw preparations, with Mediterranean and South American influences. Ceviches, carpaccios, tartares and tatakis give the list a different register.

Opéra location. The restaurant lists service Monday to Saturday. Check before crossing town, gari.

Official source
20-22 avenue Saint-Jean-Baptiste

Lou Balico

For certified Niçoise cooking, without spending dinner explaining what merda de can means.

Socca, petits farcis, merda de can and other local classics. Lou Balico carries the Cuisine Nissarde label and embraces the popular institution rather than the precious little plate.

20-22 avenue Saint-Jean-Baptiste. The bistro also offers a quicker option and takeaway for some specialities.

Official source
12 rue Lascaris, Port

JAN

For the evening when you want the white tablecloth without leaving Nice.

Jan Hendrik van der Westhuizen blends French and South African influences in a very intimate dining room. The restaurant says its produce is sourced within a 500 km radius and it has been in the Michelin Guide since 2016.

12 rue Lascaris. The restaurant lists twenty seats. Turning up without a booking with six friends is therefore a cagade, not spontaneity.

Official source
60 boulevard Franck Pilatte

Le Plongeoir

For one of those occasions when a spectacular view accompanies a place actually worth considering.

Perched above the sea on its emblematic rock, Le Plongeoir serves seasonal Mediterranean-inspired food. Yes, the picture is spectacular. No, the kitchen is still expected to do its job.

Open daily from noon to 9:30 pm during its 2026 season, listed from March to mid-November.

Official source

You can obviously eat elsewhere. Nice will survive. But if the menu is laminated, translated into six languages and being waved at you from the pavement, do not say nobody warned you.
