RESTAURANTS • MENTON

Where to eat in Menton

Menton offers something unusually useful: you can move from serious fine dining to pizza by the sea without leaving the same culinary orbit. Three moods, three choices. Twelve would not improve matters.

MAMETAS SAYS

Choose the level of ceremony before the restaurant: big night, grill with a view or very good pizza. Your wallet deserves a vote too.

30 avenue Aristide Briand

Mirazur

Choose it for: the big night. The real one, not merely the evening when you wore a white shirt.

Mauro Colagreco’s three-star restaurant builds its cooking closely around its gardens. It is a gastronomic destination in itself, not somewhere you pick because it happens to be on the way.

The catch: budget and advance planning. Reservations are released in stages and the official site publishes upcoming booking windows.

Official source
80 bis boulevard de Garavan

Casa Fuego

Choose it for: wood fire, Argentina and a distinctly less ceremonial evening.

This Mauro Colagreco address revolves around Argentinian grilling and asado, up in Garavan with views towards the Mediterranean.

The catch: it is less central than Les Sablettes and the old town. Check current opening days with the group before heading uphill.

Official source
Quai Gordon Bennet, Les Sablettes

La Pecoranegra

Choose it for: serious Neapolitan-style pizza that slots neatly after the beach or an old-town wander.

Created in Menton in 2019, Mauro Colagreco’s pizzeria presents itself as local and committed, with Neapolitan-style dough and a location directly by Les Sablettes.

The catch: this is the most casual of the three, so do not come looking for an occasion dinner. The restaurant lists daily opening from June to September; outside summer, check the weekly closing day.

Official source

Fast decision: destination dinner → Mirazur. Meat and wood fire → Casa Fuego. Post-beach and very good pizza → La Pecoranegra.
