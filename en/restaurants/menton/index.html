RESTAURANTS • MENTON

Where to eat in Menton

Six places, from a traditional town-centre table to destination dining. Menton does not need an encyclopaedia, but it deserves more than four versions of the same meal.

MAMETAS SAYS

Choose the job of the meal first: town centre, terrace, pizza, wood fire or the big experience. The name comes second.

Price: € simple · €€ moderate by Riviera standards · €€€ expensive · €€€€ big night. Relative positioning, not a live quote.

3 rue de la Marne · €€ · Mediterranean / Japanese · town centre

L’Orangerie

Choose it for: serious cooking without entering big-night territory.

Local, seasonal produce with Mediterranean cooking and Japanese accents. The house menu was listed at €39 when checked.

The catch: understated setting. Fine. The plate does the work.

Official source
14 avenue Boyer · €€ · bistronomic · opposite Jardins Biovès

Le Bistrot des Jardins

Choose it for: polished French cooking that fits easily into a central Menton day.

The restaurant works in a bistronomic register with quality produce and weekly suggestions. Lunch started at €34 when checked.

Official source
3 rue Magenta · €€ · traditional French · small square

À l’Ombre du Clocher

Choose it for: seasonal, home-made cooking in a more local and traditional register.

A small dining room and terrace on Place Loredan Larchey. Useful when you simply want lunch in Menton, not a certified gastronomic memory.

Official source
Quai Gordon Bennet, Les Sablettes · €€ · pizza · casual

La Pecoranegra

Choose it for: serious pizza after the beach or old town.

Mauro Colagreco’s pizzeria works in a Neapolitan spirit right by Les Sablettes.

Official source
80 bis boulevard de Garavan · €€€ · Argentinian grill · view

Casa Fuego

Choose it for: wood fire, asado and a view over Menton in a warm, sociable setting.

Mauro Colagreco’s Garavan table revolves around Argentinian grilling and wood-fire cooking.

The catch: less central. Go uphill because you chose it, not by accident.

Official source
30 avenue Aristide Briand · €€€€ · creative · destination dining

Mirazur

Choose it for: the big night. The actual one.

Mauro Colagreco’s cooking is closely tied to the gardens, the seasons and natural cycles.

The catch: budget and planning. This is not a table to squeeze between two sights.

Official source

20-second decision: precise central cooking - L’Orangerie. Easy bistro - Bistrot des Jardins. Traditional without ceremony - À l’Ombre du Clocher. After the beach - Pecoranegra. Wood fire and view - Casa Fuego. Gastronomic experience - Mirazur.
