RESTAURANTS • MENTON

Where to eat in Menton

Four places, four levels of ceremony. Here budget is part of the decision, not something discovered when the bill arrives.

MAMETAS SAYS

Menton does not need fifteen names. It needs choices that do different jobs: town-centre cooking, pizza, wood fire or a destination dinner.

Price: € simple · €€ moderate by Riviera standards · €€€ expensive · €€€€ big night. Relative positioning, not a live quote: summer knows exactly what it does to restaurant bills.

3 rue de la Marne · €€ · Mediterranean / Japanese · town centre

L’Orangerie

Choose it for: serious cooking without entering big-night territory.

A 2026 Michelin Bib Gourmand, this discreet central restaurant gives Mediterranean produce a precise Japanese inflection, with a terrace on a pedestrian street.

The catch: understated setting. Fine. Most of your money is going onto the plate.

Verified source
Quai Gordon Bennet, Les Sablettes · €€ · pizza · casual

La Pecoranegra

Choose it for: serious pizza after the beach or old town.

Mauro Colagreco’s pizzeria works in a Neapolitan spirit right by Les Sablettes.

The catch: very casual. For the anniversary dinner, keep reading.

Official source
80 bis boulevard de Garavan · €€€ · Argentinian grill · view

Casa Fuego

Choose it for: wood fire and a less ceremonial evening than Mirazur.

Mauro Colagreco’s Garavan address revolves around Argentinian grilling and asado.

The catch: less central; check current services before heading uphill.

Official source
30 avenue Aristide Briand · €€€€ · creative · destination dining

Mirazur

Choose it for: the big night. The actual one.

Mauro Colagreco holds three Michelin stars in 2026, with cooking closely tied to the gardens and natural cycles.

The catch: budget and planning. This is not a table to squeeze between two sights.

Official source

20-second decision: best cooking/value balance → L’Orangerie. After the beach → Pecoranegra. Wood fire and view → Casa Fuego. Gastronomic experience → Mirazur.
