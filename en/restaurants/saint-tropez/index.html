Where to eat in Saint-Tropez

Eight places covering several versions of Saint-Tropez: an accessible Provençal lunch, Ponche seafood, harbour dining, a lively dinner or an evening that becomes the programme.

MAMETAS SAYS

In Saint-Tropez, price is part of the scenery. Choose the level of ceremony before you sit down.

Price: € simple · €€ moderate locally · €€€ expensive · €€€€ big night. Relative and highly seasonal positioning.

Centre · €€ · Provençal / Italian · friendly

La Part des Anges

Choose it for: a central lunch that is genuinely more accessible than the Saint-Tropez average.

Provençal and Italian cooking, daily suggestions and a small terrace. The tourism office listed a two-course lunch menu at €27 when checked.

The catch: only twenty seats. Lower price does not mean backup plan.

3 rue du Temple · €€ · Provençal / wood fire · garden

La Ramade

Choose it for: market-day lunch or a wood-fired dinner behind Place des Lices.

Meat and fish over wood fire in a warm garden. The restaurant is seasonal, which suits it.

Official sourceOpen map ↗
8 rue des Remparts · €€ · seafood · Ponche

Le Petit Pointu

Choose it for: fish in the old Ponche quarter.

A family seafood table with grilled fish, calamari, sardines and seafood pasta.

Official sourceOpen map ↗
5 rue des Remparts · €€€ · Mediterranean · historic

La Ponche

Choose it for: dinner inside the historical Saint-Tropez myth.

The hotel restaurant reaches towards the tiny Ponche cove in a house tied to Boris Vian, Bardot and the Nouvelle Vague.

The catch: part of the bill pays for the setting. At least this one has a history.

Official sourceOpen map ↗
1 chemin des Conquettes · €€€ · Moroccan / Mediterranean · lively

Salama

Choose it for: a complete change of register and a later, livelier dinner.

Home-made Moroccan and Mediterranean cooking near Place des Lices.

Quai Jean Jaurès · €€€ · seafood · harbour

Le Girelier

Choose it for: shellfish, fish and bouillabaisse on the harbour, institution edition.

A seafood house here for more than fifty years. Yes, it is on the harbour. The harbour is not a disease.

Colette

Choose it for: a big dinner away from the harbour.

Philippe Colinet works with highly seasonal, plant-forward cooking, dinner only.

The catch: occasion dining, not an improvised supper.

Official sourceOpen map ↗
Cheval Blanc · €€€€ · gastronomic · destination

La Vague d’Or

Choose it for: the very big night.

Arnaud Donckele builds a gastronomic vision of Provence between land and sea. This is the evening's programme, not a restaurant squeezed between two sights.
