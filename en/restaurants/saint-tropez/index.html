RESTAURANTS • SAINT-TROPEZ

Where to eat in Saint-Tropez

In Saint-Tropez, the bill can occasionally have more personality than the food. These five places have a reason to exist beyond the valet parking.

LES NIÇOISES SAY

For a Tropezian meal without the celebrity circus, start in the Ponche quarter or with seafood. For a major dinner, the stars have earned their place.

8 rue des Remparts

Le Petit Pointu

Pour le poisson dans le vieux quartier de la Ponche.

A family-run seafood table whose current identity dates to 2015, with grilled fish, calamari, sardines and seafood pasta in a deliberately maritime setting.

Official source
5 rue des Remparts

La Ponche

Pour dîner littéralement au bord du mythe tropézien.

The five-star hotel’s Mediterranean restaurant extends towards the tiny Ponche cove, in a house whose history is tied to Boris Vian, Bardot and the Nouvelle Vague. The setting is famous; the reason to go is that it still feels like a place, not a theme park.

Official source
Quai Jean Jaurès

Le Girelier

Pour les produits de la mer sur le port, version institution.

A Saint-Tropez seafood house on the port for more than fifty years, with shellfish, grilled fish, bouillabaisse and the full Mediterranean repertoire. Yes, it is on the port. No, that does not automatically make it a tourist trap.

Official source
151 route des Salins, Hôtel Sezz

Colette

Pour une étoile Michelin loin du port.

Chef Philippe Colinet runs this dinner-only Michelin-starred restaurant at Hôtel Sezz, with a highly seasonal, plant-forward Mediterranean approach. A good option when you want Saint-Tropez to lower its voice for a few hours.

Official source
Plage de la Bouillabaisse, Cheval Blanc

La Vague d’Or

Pour le grand soir. Très grand.

Arnaud Donckele’s three-Michelin-star restaurant builds an intensely Mediterranean cuisine around local fish, vegetables and extraordinary sauces. This is not the place to “grab something”. It is the evening.

Official source
