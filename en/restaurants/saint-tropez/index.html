Where to eat in Saint-Tropez

Seven places covering several versions of Saint-Tropez: Ponche seafood, market-day lunch, Moroccan dinner, harbour institution, Michelin star or the very big night.

MAMETAS SAYS

Here, price is part of the scenery. You may as well choose it deliberately.

3 rue du Temple · €€ · Provençal / wood fire · garden

La Ramade

Choose it for: market-day lunch or a wood-fired dinner just behind Place des Lices.

Meat and fish over wood fire in a warm garden. The tourism office lists daily opening from 1 May to 25 October 2026, with lunch on Tuesdays and Saturdays.

The catch: seasonal. Which is partly why it still feels local rather than exportable.

Official source
8 rue des Remparts · €€ · seafood · Ponche

Le Petit Pointu

Choose it for: fish in the old Ponche quarter.

A family seafood table with grilled fish, calamari, sardines and seafood pasta.

Official source
5 rue des Remparts · €€€ · Mediterranean · historic

La Ponche

Choose it for: dinner right inside the Saint-Tropez myth.

The hotel restaurant reaches towards the tiny Ponche cove in a house tied to Boris Vian, Bardot and the Nouvelle Vague.

The catch: part of the bill clearly pays for the setting. At least this one has a history.

Official source
1 chemin des Conquettes · €€€ · Moroccan / Mediterranean · lively

Salama

Choose it for: a complete change of register and a later, livelier dinner.

Home-made Moroccan and Mediterranean cooking near Place des Lices. The restaurant currently advertises evening services at 8pm and 10.30pm.

The catch: not your quiet Provençal evening. That is the point.

Official source
Quai Jean Jaurès · €€€ · seafood · harbour

Le Girelier

Choose it for: shellfish, fish and bouillabaisse on the harbour, institution edition.

A seafood house here for more than fifty years. Yes, it is on the harbour. The harbour is not a disease.

Official source
Hôtel Sezz · €€€€ · Mediterranean · Michelin-starred

Colette

Choose it for: a Michelin star away from the harbour.

Philippe Colinet serves highly seasonal, plant-forward cooking at dinner.

The catch: occasion dining, not an improvised supper.

Official source
Cheval Blanc, Plage de la Bouillabaisse · €€€€ · gastronomic · destination

La Vague d’Or

Choose it for: the very big night.

Arnaud Donckele’s three-star table builds Mediterranean cooking around fish, vegetables and sauces. This is not “where shall we eat?” so much as “what are we doing with the evening?”

Verified source

20-second decision: market day → La Ramade. Old-town fish → Petit Pointu. Historic setting → La Ponche. Lively dinner → Salama. Harbour seafood → Girelier. Quieter Michelin star → Colette. Destination night → Vague d’Or.
