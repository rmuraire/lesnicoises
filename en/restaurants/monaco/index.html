Where to eat in Monaco

In Monaco, the bill can become a fashion accessory. We prefer deciding why you are sitting down first: unfussy seafood in Fontvieille, or a proper dinner in Monte-Carlo.

MAMETAS SAYS

Two places cover two genuinely different moods. If neither fits your evening, change the plan rather than collect more names.

Quai Jean-Charles Rey, Port de Fontvieille

Les Perles de Monte-Carlo

Choose it for: a seafood lunch, a casual mood and the Monaco that is not trying quite so hard to be Monaco.

Brice Cachia and Frédéric Rouxeville, both trained biologists, started refining marine products here in 2011. Come for the house oysters, shellfish and wooden benches in Fontvieille.

The catch: this is not your dressed-up grand dinner. The house states that booking is required; check current services before going.

Official source
3 Avenue des Spélugues, Monte-Carlo

Rampoldi Monte-Carlo

Choose it for: an elegant dinner in central Monte-Carlo, when you want to embrace the setting rather than escape it.

Open since 1946, Rampoldi serves refined Italian cooking with Mediterranean influences. It is the more formal choice here, close to the Carré d’Or.

The catch: setting and service are part of the proposition. The restaurant currently lists daily opening; booking is sensible.

Official source

Fast decision: casual, briny lunch → Les Perles. Smarter dinner in central Monte-Carlo → Rampoldi. There, that was painless.
