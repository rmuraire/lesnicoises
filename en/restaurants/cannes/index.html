Where to eat in Cannes

Ten addresses to avoid two Cannes traps: choosing only for the view, or only for the price. Both matter. So does the food, rather inconveniently.

MAMETAS SAYS

Cannes can be expensive. That is not the problem. The problem starts when the bill has had more thought than the cooking.

80 rue Meynadier · Forville · €€

Aux Bons Enfants

Provençal · Family-run · Best for: local cooking beside Forville Market.

A family house since 1935, now in its fourth generation, with local produce closely tied to the market. The catch: the house does not accept credit cards; bring cash or a cheque.

Official source
5 rue Jean Daumas · €€ · small dining room

La Table du Chef

Best for: a calm dinner built around a surprise menu.

Four blind courses shaped by the best current produce and local suppliers. The catch: you hand part of the decision to the chef. That is the concept.

Official source
12 avenue Saint-Louis · €€ · traditional · terrace

Côté Jardin by Steve Moracchini

Best for: lunch or dinner away from Croisette overstatement.

A compact dining room and terrace, with a 2026 daily menu listed at €27 by Cannes Tourism. The catch: less icon, more actual meal.

Cannes Tourism
4 rue Émile Négrin · €€ · modern · counter

Le Pompon

Best for: small plates to share and a current evening without palace pricing.

A daily-changing board built around fresh market produce and a selected wine list. The catch: more gourmet counter than Cannes institution.

Official source
5 rue Lafontaine · €€ · classic · polished bistro

L’Affable

Best for: classic French cooking driven by product and seasonality.

Didier Chouteau keeps the cooking elegant and readable in a warm setting. The catch: no sea view to monetise on Instagram. A survivable tragedy.

Official source
22 rue Saint-Antoine · Le Suquet · €€€

Table 22 by Noël Mantel

Traditional / Mediterranean · Elegant · Best for: a serious dinner in Le Suquet.

A more worked experience than the classic bistro while remaining rooted in the neighbourhood. The catch: occasion budget, not a casual stop between trains.

Cannes Tourism
27 rue Félix Faure · Forville · €€€

Astoux et Brun

Seafood · Busy institution · Best for: shellfish and crustaceans.

One of Cannes’ historic seafood houses. The catch: more institution than intimate date night.

Cannes Tourism
6 rue Florian · €€€ · Mediterranean · stylish / lively

La Môme

Best for: when dinner also needs to be the night out.

The house deliberately leans into glamour, music and energy. The catch: if you want monastic silence, you have chosen the wrong film.

Official source
Carlton Cannes · Croisette · €€€€

Riviera

Mediterranean brasserie · Palace · Best for: a polished lunch or dinner with a view.

Seasonality, local sourcing and fresh catch on a terrace above the Croisette. The catch: go because you want the palace register, not despite it.

Official source
Hôtel Martinez · Croisette · €€€€

La Palme d’Or

Fish / Mediterranean · Fine dining · Best for: the Cannes big night.

Sea-driven cooking, raw or wood-grilled fish and a deliberately sunnier, less stiff fine-dining mood. The catch: time, budget and booking. Come on purpose.

Official source
