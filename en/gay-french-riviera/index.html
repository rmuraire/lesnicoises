FRENCH RIVIERA · LGBTQ+ TRAVEL

Gay French Riviera: the useful version

The useful question is not “where is the gay Riviera?” It is “where should I base myself, and how do I use the coast without turning the whole trip into LGBTQ+ logistics?”

Best single base: NiceStrong transport, a visible LGBTQ+ ecosystem and the easiest access to both sides of the coast.
West: Antibes + CannesEasy to fold into the trip from Nice; worth a different base only if the western Riviera is your main event.
East: Monaco + MentonSimple train days from Nice. No need to invent a separate LGBTQ+ itinerary for each stop.

Nice is where the LGBTQ+ infrastructure on this stretch of the Riviera is most explicit. The tourism board runs Nice Rainbow, belongs to IGLTA and reported 80 labelled establishments in its 2026 material. That is why Mametas uses Nice as the anchor for this guide. The rest of the coast is then a geography problem, not an identity problem.

THE MAMETAS TAKE

For a first trip, sleep in Nice and travel out. It gives you the community layer when you want it, while keeping Villefranche, Cap-Ferrat, Antibes, Cannes, Monaco and Menton within easy reach.

If you want one base, choose Nice

Nice makes the most sense when you want a no-car trip, a city that still works at night and the option of explicitly LGBTQ+ venues without building the whole holiday around them. When Cannes makes more sense

Cannes becomes a better base if your trip is mostly about the western Riviera: Antibes, the Lérins Islands, Cannes itself and perhaps Saint-Paul-de-Vence or the Esterel. It is more polished and less practical as an LGBTQ+ anchor than Nice, but geography can still win. If half your saved places are west of Nice, do not commute across the coast just to obey a guide.

Saint-Tropez: treat it as a detour, not the backbone

Saint-Tropez is the least useful place here for building a first no-car Riviera itinerary. Go because you want Saint-Tropez — the village, Pampelonne, the scene — not because you expect it to organise the rest of the coast for you. For most first-time travellers, it works better as a deliberate western detour than as the main LGBTQ+ base.

See the Mametas Saint-Tropez guide →

Monaco and Menton: easy eastbound days

Monaco and Menton fit naturally together on the eastern side of a Nice-based trip. The practical point is precisely that you do not need a special gay plan for them. Use Nice for the LGBTQ+ infrastructure; use Monaco and Menton for what they are actually good at.

Villefranche and Cap-Ferrat: the reset days

These are the days when the LGBTQ+ label becomes irrelevant. Go for the bay, the coastal paths, the water and the slower rhythm. If your whole itinerary is starting to look like a directory, this is the cure.
