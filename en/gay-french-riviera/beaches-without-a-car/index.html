Gay beaches around Nice without a car

Three very different answers: one easy and serviced, one rocky and local, one genuinely wild. The word “gay beach” hides rather a lot of logistics.

Easiest: CastelCentral Nice, Nice Rainbow-labelled, paid loungers and services.
Most local: Coco BeachRocky public coast east of the Port; community reputation, no formal gay zoning.
Most explicit: Saint-Laurent-d’ÈzeLong-established gay/naturist coves, but stairs, rocks and zero glamour in the access.
THE MAMETAS TAKE

Choose Castel for ease, Coco for an uncomplicated local swim, Saint-Laurent-d’Èze for the actual gay/naturist-beach experience. Do not choose the last one in flip-flops and then blame the Mediterranean.

Castel Plage — the easy answer

At the eastern end of Nice’s seafront, Castel is a private beach with restaurant, loungers, umbrellas and lifeguarded swimming. The official Nice Côte d’Azur listing currently carries the Nice Rainbow label. Specialist gay guides also describe it as a long-standing LGBTQ+ gathering point.

No-car verdict: excellent. Walk from Old Nice or use the city tram/bus network and finish on foot. You are paying for comfort and location, not secrecy.

Coco Beach — free, rocky, less formal

Coco Beach is not a conventional sandy beach and not an officially designated gay beach. It is the rocky public coastline east of the Port, with a long-standing gay/naturist reputation in specialist guides. Come for the water and the setting, not for services.

No-car verdict: good if you are comfortable walking from the Port and moving over rock. Bring water and proper footwear.

Saint-Laurent-d’Èze — the one that needs instructions

The coves beyond Saint-Laurent-d’Èze are the most clearly established gay/naturist option in this part of the Riviera. They are also the least casual to reach. The public beach itself is reached by stairs and a tunnel below the railway; the naturist/gay coves continue over a rough coastal path.

Recent September 2026 visitor reports point to the 607 bus from Nice and the stop “Parking Saint-Laurent d’Èze”. Route numbers on this coast have changed before, so verify the same day with ZOU! or your live map rather than trusting a five-year-old blog post.

Mobility reality: many stairs, uneven rock, no useful services at the gay/naturist coves. This is not the beach to improvise with limited mobility or bad footwear.

Which one should you choose?

First gay Riviera tripCastel first. Learn the coast before turning access into a sport.
Free afternoon in NiceCoco Beach if the sea matters more than a lounger.
You specifically want naturismSaint-Laurent-d’Èze, with time and sensible shoes.

What to take

Water shoes for pebbles and rocks, drinking water, sun protection and something more substantial than flip-flops for Saint-Laurent-d’Èze. On the private beaches, a credit card solves much of this. On the wild coves, it solves almost nothing.
