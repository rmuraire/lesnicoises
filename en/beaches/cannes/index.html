BEACHES • CANNES

Cannes beaches: choose the right strip of sand

Yes, Cannes has actual sand. No, you do not need a palace beach club to touch it. The useful question is which part of the bay fits your day.

WEST CROISETTE • BY THE PALAIS

Brigitte Bardot beach

The central public answer.

The former Macé beach was renamed Brigitte Bardot beach in May 2026. It is the large public beach on the western Croisette, close to the Palais des Festivals and surrounded by private hotel beaches. This is the easiest sand if you are already in central Cannes and do not want the day to become logistics.

TypeFree public sandy beach.
Best forCentral convenience, a quick swim, families staying around the Palais.
MèfiVery popular in summer. Central does not mean secret.
LandmarkNear the Palais des Festivals and Majestic end of the Croisette.

CROISETTE • MUNICIPAL BEACH

Zamenhof

A sunbed without palace-beach pricing.

Cannes runs Zamenhof itself beside the Martinez. In summer it offers supervised swimming, showers, changing cabins, accessibility equipment, loungers and parasols at municipal prices.

2026 seasonMid-June to mid-September, 8:30 am-6:30 pm.
Lounger€6.50 half-day or €9 full day.
Parasol€4.50 half-day or €8 full day.
ReserveThe City operates online booking during the season.

POINTE CROISETTE • SQUARE VERDUN

Bijou Plage

The family and accessibility choice.

At the eastern end of the Croisette, between Port Canto and Palm Beach, Bijou has shallow access that Cannes Tourism specifically recommends for families. The municipal Handiplage is here too.

FamilyGentle entry into the water and supervised swimming in peak season.
AccessibilityHandiplage operates from 15 June to 15 September 2026 with adapted equipment and trained staff; eligibility and booking rules apply.
TransportBus stop: Bijou Plage.

EAST CANNES • GAZAGNAIRE / MOURÉ ROUGE

Mouré Rouge

When you want Cannes without sitting under a palace.

Past Palm Beach, the Gazagnaire and Mouré Rouge stretch feels more residential and local. Cannes Tourism describes these as family beaches close to the small Mouré Rouge port and water-sports base.

WEST OF THE OLD PORT • BOULEVARD DU MIDI

Boulevard du Midi

More space, less Croisette theatre.

The public sandy beaches continue west from the old port towards La Bocca. They are more family-oriented, generally less crowded than the Croisette and face the Estérel and Lérins Islands. If your aim is an actual beach day rather than a Cannes symbol, start here.

And the private beaches?

The Croisette has many hotel and concession beaches. Choose one because you want the restaurant, service or atmosphere, not because private sand is somehow a different mineral. If the beach club itself is the plan in July or August, reserve. Otherwise the public beaches above are perfectly legitimate answers.

For the best swimming day from Cannes: consider the Lérins Islands. They are 15-20 minutes by boat and give you coves, clear water and a completely different rhythm from the Croisette.
