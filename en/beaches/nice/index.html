Where to swim in Nice : the beaches to choose

Nice does not have a secret beach, gari. It is a big city with an international airport. If TikTok “revealed” it to you, the secret is already dead.

Mametas CheckedPractical information checked · September 2026
PebblesAlong the main seafront. Your towel did nothing to deserve this.
Smoke-freeAll Nice beaches have been smoke-free since 2024.
9 amIn August, the time when a beach is still a beach rather than a public meeting.

You want white sand, clear water, dégun around and all of it five minutes from Old Nice in August? Calu. A beach with hundreds of Google reviews, a bus stop and twelve Reels filmed before breakfast stopped being secret some time ago. Pick the right stretch of coast and stop looking for Narnia.

QUAI DES ÉTATS-UNIS

Ponchettes

The convenient one.

Right below Old Nice and Castle Hill. You can leave the water and be at Cours Saleya minutes later. You, half the city and several thousand visitors have all noticed the same useful fact. Try not to look personally betrayed when you see each other.

AccessQuai des États-Unis, between Old Nice, Cours Saleya and Castle Hill. This is the central swim by definition.
Lifeguards 2026The station runs to 13 September: 9 am–6:30 pm in July and August, then 9 am–6 pm in September.
Getting outAn access rope helps with the pebble slope until 30 September. For a fully adapted Handiplage, use Centenaire or Carras.
Next doorCours Saleya is a few minutes away; the historic Ponchettes buildings also host contemporary-art exhibition spaces.

Find it. Tram T1 · Opéra–Vieille Ville. Open map ↗

8 QUAI DES ÉTATS-UNIS · BELOW CASTLE HILL

Castel Plage

The Nice institution.

At the eastern end of Quai des États-Unis, directly below Castle Hill, Castel is the iconic private-beach version of Nice: sunbeds, Mediterranean restaurant and the Baie des Anges filling the frame. The public Castel beach sits immediately beside it, so swimming here does not require renting a mattress.

BeachCastel currently lists the beach as open daily from 9 am to 6 pm.
RestaurantCurrent service hours: noon–4 pm and 7:30–9:30 pm.
2026 seasonThe Tourist Office lists the venue daily from 1 May to 1 October.
ServicesSunbeds, parasols, restaurant and private events. Booking is sensible in peak summer.

Find it. Tram T1 · Opéra–Vieille Ville, then follow Quai des États-Unis east. Map ↗ · Official site ↗

107 QUAI DES ÉTATS-UNIS

Beau Rivage

The central beach club with almost no logistical drama.

Facing Old Nice, Beau Rivage combines beach, mattresses, restaurant and lounge. It is less “secret cove” than a day engineered to be easy: Cours Saleya is close and lunch does not require putting your shoes back on.

OpeningOpen year-round with seasonal hours. From 20 September to 31 December 2026, the Tourist Office lists 10 am–6 pm.
RestaurantFor that period: noon–3 pm. Summer hours run later.
ServicesSupervised beach, sunbeds, parasols, wifi, watersports and restaurant.
AccessibilityThe Tourist Office lists wheelchair access with assistance.

Find it. Tram T1 · Opéra–Vieille Ville. Map ↗ · Official site ↗

47 PROMENADE DES ANGLAIS

Plage Amour

The beach that turns into a music venue.

Plage Amour is now a genuine cultural and nightlife address on the seafront, not simply a line of sunbeds. Come for lunch, drinks or the day; in summer, its concert and DJ programme attracts plenty of people who had no previous intention of spending ten hours under a parasol.

HoursOpen daily from 9 am to midnight; the official site lists continuous kitchen service until 11 pm.
2026 festivalThe season included Wet Leg, Feu! Chatterton, Polo & Pan, Honey Dijon, Sébastien Tellier and Kavinsky.
ServicesRestaurant, bar, sunbeds, beach-equipment rental and private events.
LabelGreen Key 2026.

Find it. Tram T2 · Alsace-Lorraine, then walk down to the Promenade. Map ↗ · Official site ↗

BOULEVARD FRANCK PILATTE

La Réserve

Smaller, further east and less Promenade.

A good option if you are combining the Port, Cap de Nice and a swim. It feels more local without becoming an anthropological expedition.

AccessFrom Port Lympia, continue east along boulevard Franck Pilatte; bus 98 also follows this stretch of coastline.
TypeA small public pebble beach at the foot of Cap de Nice.
Combine it withThe Port, coastal path and Coco Beach all fit naturally into the same outing.
Next doorLa Réserve restaurant and its panoramic bar sit above the water if the swim suddenly requires a tablecloth.

Find it. Tram T2 · Port Lympia, then walk or take bus 98. Open map ↗

CAP DE NICE

Coco Beach

For rocks and clear water, not for constructing a small private estate around your towel.

A rocky swimming area by the coastal path. Nice city authorities specifically warn against diving from the rocks here. Do not be the niocou doing it for a video. Gravity remains one of the Riviera’s more reliable institutions.

AccessBeyond La Réserve, by Jardin Félix Rainaud and the coastal path. Uneven ground and steps: wear actual shoes.
FeelRocks, small swimming areas and a coastline far wilder than the Promenade.
SafetyThe City explicitly warns against diving from the rocks at Coco Beach.
NextContinue along the coastal path towards the Cap if the stairs have not yet offended you.

Find it. Tram T2 · Port Lympia, then bus 98 or walk along Franck Pilatte. Open map ↗

PROMENADE DES ANGLAIS · OPPOSITE JARDIN ALBERT-Ier

Centenaire

The central answer when accessibility matters.

Centenaire remains a very central public beach, but its most useful distinction is one most “best beach” lists forget: the City operates an adapted Handiplage here. If somebody in your group has mobility needs, that matters more than an Instagram ranking.

Find it. Tram T1 · Masséna or Opéra–Vieille Ville depending on where you start. Open map ↗

HU, WE HAVE EXPLAINED THIS

For a quick swim: Ponchettes. For a proper sea break: La Réserve or Coco Beach. For dégun around in August: February. We are Niçoises, pichoun, not magicians.

Two useful things

Nice states that all city beaches have been smoke-free since 2024. In summer 2026 several lifeguard stations operate from 9 am to 6:30 pm in July and August, with selected beaches continuing into September. Check on the day if supervised swimming matters to you.

And buy water shoes if you dislike pebbles. Mèfi, vanity has sent better people limping back across the Promenade. The pebbles were here before your pedicure and do not give a damn about your aesthetic objections.
