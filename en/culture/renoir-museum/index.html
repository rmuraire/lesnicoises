Renoir Museum

Renoir came south for the light. He ended up finding an olive-tree estate where he spent his final years and kept painting.

MAMETAS SAYS

This is not the Riviera’s most spectacular museum. That is part of the point: a house, a garden and a painter placed back inside the landscape that kept him here.

Why go

The Collettes estate was Pierre-Auguste Renoir’s last home. The visit makes sense as much for the setting of his final years as for the works, objects, furniture and photographs kept there.

What to look at

The rooms, the studio and especially the garden with its century-old olive trees. The connection between painting and landscape becomes much less abstract here.

PRACTICAL

19 chemin des Collettes, Cagnes-sur-Mer. Closed Tuesdays; opening hours vary by season. Check the day’s schedule before going.

Pair it with Haut-de-Cagnes. From Nice, give it a proper half-day rather than treating it as a drive-by museum.
