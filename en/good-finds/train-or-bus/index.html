Train or bus?

On the coast, start with the train. For hilltop villages and the awkward last mile, buses suddenly become much more intelligent.

If the destination has a coastal station, check TER first. If the place is perched above the railway, stop being loyal to the train and look at the bus.

The simple rule

COASTAL AXIS

Nice, Antibes, Cannes, Monaco, Menton: train first

TER is usually the cleanest answer for the main coastal towns. It avoids traffic, puts you in the centre and runs frequently enough that you do not need to build the day around one departure.

Book / check live times. TER Sud ↗ · SNCF Connect ↗

HILLTOP / INLAND

Èze Village, Saint-Paul-de-Vence and inland stops: bus matters

Èze railway station is down by the sea, not in the medieval village. Saint-Paul-de-Vence has no station at all. That is where the regional bus network earns its place in the trip.

Useful examples: ZOU! line 602 serves Nice, Èze Village and Monaco. Line 655 links Cagnes-sur-Mer, Saint-Paul-de-Vence and Vence.

Typical TER budget

Antibes: about €7. Cannes: about €9.70. Monaco: about €6.10. Menton: about €7.70.

Nice to Antibes averages around 20 minutes. Nice to Cannes is roughly 35 minutes. Monaco to Nice is also around 20 minutes on the current official timetable pages.

Nice itself: tram and bus, not TER

Inside Nice, use Lignes d'Azur. The current Solo fare is €1.70 and includes transfers for 74 minutes. The current passes are €7 for 1 day, €13 for 2 days and €20 for 7 days. A rechargeable anonymous card costs €2 and can be topped up at tram machines and other points of sale.

The pass that can actually make sense

The PASS Sud Azur Explore is worth checking if you are moving a lot. It covers trains, trams and buses across the Alpes-Maritimes and Monaco: €35 for 3 days, €50 for 7 days and €80 for 14 days for an adult.

The Mametas calculation: if you are doing several TER day trips plus urban transport, price the pass before buying each journey separately. If you are mostly walking around Nice with one day trip, individual tickets may still be cheaper.

Three mistakes to avoid

  • Do not take the train to Èze-sur-Mer assuming you have arrived in Èze Village.
  • Do not use a regional bus for Nice to Cannes just because buses feel simpler. The TER is usually the cleaner default.
  • Do not buy a transport pass because it sounds efficient. Count the trips you will actually make.
