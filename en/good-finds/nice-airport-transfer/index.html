GOOD FINDS • ARRIVAL LOGISTICS

Nice Airport → where you sleep

Tram, train or taxi? The airport is not the difficult part, pichoun. The cagade is choosing transport before deciding where you are sleeping.

Staying in central Nice? Take the tram.

For most first-time visitors sleeping in central Nice, tram line 2 is the boringly sensible answer. It runs from both terminals into the city centre and on to Port Lympia. No station detour, no rental-car desk, no ceremonial first traffic jam.

If your hotel is in Old Nice, around Jean Médecin, the Promenade or near the port, check the nearest tram stop before you land. Five minutes of map-reading now beats twenty minutes of suitcase theatre later.

Antibes, Cannes, Monaco or Menton? Think train.

Nice Saint-Augustin station is the airport’s useful little secret that is not actually secret. The airport provides a free tram connection between the terminals and Grand Arénas; from there, the station is a short walk. TER trains run along the coast in both directions.

For Antibes and Cannes, head west. For Monaco and Menton, head east. This is often cleaner than crossing Nice just to use Nice-Ville station. Check the live TER timetable for your arrival day, especially late in the evening or during engineering works.

Villefranche, Beaulieu and the Cap-Ferrat question

For Villefranche-sur-Mer or Beaulieu-sur-Mer, the coastal train is usually the first option to inspect. Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat is different: the peninsula is not sitting obediently on the railway line. Depending on the hotel, luggage and arrival time, a taxi for the final leg can be the intelligent indulgence.

When a taxi is the right answer

Heavy luggage, children, a late arrival, a hillside hotel or four people splitting the fare: take the taxi if it removes genuine friction. Mametas does not award medals for dragging a suitcase through three connections to save a few euros.

Do check the airport’s current official taxi information before travelling. Rates and conditions can change; our job is to help you choose the mode, not fossilise a tariff on the internet.

And the rental car?

Do not collect one automatically because you are on the Côte d’Azur. If the first part of the trip is Nice plus the coastal railway axis, you may spend more time parking the thing than enjoying it. Rent a car when the itinerary actually needs one — the inland villages, a road-trip section or places poorly served by public transport — not because the arrivals hall has a desk.

The Mametas rule: Nice centre → tram. Antibes, Cannes, Monaco or Menton → inspect Saint-Augustin + TER first. Heavy luggage, hills or a difficult arrival time → taxi. Inland road trip → car when you need it, not three days earlier.

Check these before you land

Transport details move. Verify the day’s service on the official Nice Airport public-transport page, the airport’s train-access page and TER Sud Provence-Alpes-Côte d’Azur. Then screenshot your hotel address. Voilà. Arrival solved.
