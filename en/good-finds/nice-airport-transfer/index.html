Nice Airport: get to your base

Tram, train, coach or taxi? Choose by where you sleep. The first transport decision should be boring.

MAMETAS SAYS

Nice centre: tram. Coastal town with a station: check Saint-Augustin plus TER. Too much luggage, too many children or an awkward hotel: taxi becomes a rational purchase.

Central Nice: tram line 2

Line 2 serves both terminals, Jean Médecin and Port Lympia. Nice Airport says the ride to Jean Médecin is under 30 minutes and trams run roughly every 7 to 8 minutes on weekdays.

The dedicated Aéro ticket currently costs €10 return and includes connections. If you already have a loaded Lignes d'Azur card, the general network fare can also be used for airport access.

Antibes, Cannes, Monaco or Menton: Saint-Augustin + TER

The useful station is Nice Saint-Augustin, beside Grand Arénas. The tram is free between Terminal 2, Terminal 1 and Grand Arénas, and SNCF describes Saint-Augustin as the multimodal station built closer to the airport.

From there, trains head west towards Antibes and Cannes, and east towards Monaco and Menton. This usually makes more sense than dragging luggage into Nice-Ville just to come back out again.

Direct coach can beat the train when luggage wins

ZOU! currently lists line 80 from Nice Airport to Monaco and Menton, and line 81 from Nice Airport to Cannes. They are worth checking when you want a direct luggage-friendly ride without changing between tram and train.

Taxi: the expensive answer that can still be the correct one

Nice Airport publishes indicative fixed 2026 fares from the airport: €32 to central Nice, €72 to Antibes / Juan-les-Pins, €85 to Cannes and €95 to Monaco, excluding supplements.

Four people, late arrival, children, several bags or a hillside hotel can change the calculation very quickly. The point is not to avoid taxis. It is to avoid paying for one when the tram or train solves the problem cleanly.

Villefranche, Beaulieu and Cap-Ferrat

Villefranche-sur-Mer and Beaulieu-sur-Mer have coastal stations, so TER is the first option to inspect. Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat is different: the peninsula is not on the railway. Train to Beaulieu plus bus or taxi may work, but with luggage a direct taxi can be a very sensible indulgence.

Rental car: not on day one by reflex

If your first days are Nice plus the coastal railway axis, do not collect a rental car automatically. Rent it when the itinerary actually needs one: inland villages, road-trip sections or places where public transport introduces real friction.

The 30-second answer: Nice centre = tram. Antibes / Cannes / Monaco / Menton = Saint-Augustin + TER first. Direct airport coach if it simplifies the bags. Taxi when comfort genuinely changes the arrival. Car only when the itinerary starts needing roads.
