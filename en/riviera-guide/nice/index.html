RIVIERA GUIDE • NICE

Nice guide

Nice will welcome you perfectly well. It simply did not wait for you to exist, gari. Remember that and we will all get along.

2 daysA comfortable minimum if you would rather visit Nice than process it.
No car neededThe centre is walkable. Tram and regional trains handle the rest.
PebblesYes, still. Water shoes are less elegant than A&E, but considerably more useful.

Every summer, somebody tries to do Old Nice, Èze, Monaco and Menton before aperitivo. Ambitious. Nice is a real city, with working neighbourhoods, early markets and locals who have no desire to become extras in your stories. You are welcome; just leave enough time to notice where you are.

If you only have one day

8:30 am

Cours Saleya before everyone else

From Tuesday to Sunday the market fills Old Nice in the morning. Monday is antiques day. Go early. At noon in August you are not discovering Cours Saleya; you are attending a convention. Do not complain afterwards that nobody knows the good places anymore. You all arrived together.

10:00 am

Castle Hill

For the view over the Baie des Anges, Old Nice and the Port. There is no castle. Louis XIV dealt with that. The name survived beautifully.

12:30 pm

Eat Niçoise food, not necessarily on the seafront

Socca, petits farcis, daube, pissaladière, chard pie. A six-page menu in five languages and a waiter waving at you from the pavement are not, despite appearances, signs of local authenticity.

3:00 pm

The Port and Cap de Nice

Walk east through Port Lympia towards Franck Pilatte. The city quietens, the coastline gets rockier and the coastal path continues beyond La Réserve above Coco Beach.

5:30 pm

Swim

Ponchettes for convenience. La Réserve or Coco Beach if you want the eastern side of town. In summer, supervised beaches operate to published hours. The Mediterranean does not read them.

THE MAMETAS VERDICT

For a first Riviera trip, stay in Nice rather than Cannes. More city, more contrast, better transport. Cannes will recover. And if your plan contains four towns in forty-eight hours, that is not a holiday. It is a cagade with bookings.

If you have a second day

Cimiez

Head uphill for a different city: the Matisse Museum, Roman remains, olive gardens and the monastery gardens sit close together. The Matisse Museum is closed on Tuesdays. Useful information, particularly on a Tuesday.

Old Nice with no objective

Go back without an itinerary. An old town is perfectly capable of surviving ten minutes without Google Maps.

How many nights in Nice?

Two nights: Nice itself

Enough for a convincing first look if Nice is the destination rather than the transport hub. Keep one full day for the centre, Old Nice, the sea and one extra neighbourhood. Do not spend half of it checking the departure board for Monaco.

Three or four nights: Nice plus the Riviera

This is where the city starts to work as a base. You can keep two proper Nice days and add one or two outings — Villefranche and Cap-Ferrat, Monaco and Menton, or Antibes — without packing the suitcase again.

Five nights or more: the useful base wins

At five nights, Nice becomes less a city break and more a Riviera operating system. That is exactly why our five-day no-car plan keeps one hotel here: the railway, tram, restaurants and airport access remove enough friction that you can spend your energy on the places, not the choreography.

Where to stay in Nice: choose the friction you can live with

There is no single “best neighbourhood”. There is a best compromise for the trip you are actually taking. Sea view, station access, old-town atmosphere and quiet do not all occupy the same square metre, despite what booking filters would like you to believe.

Nice-Ville / Jean Médecin: best for train-heavy trips

If you plan several Riviera day trips, this is the practical answer. You are close to the main station, the tram and the central grid. The catch is equally clear: some streets feel more urban than postcard Nice. This is where location earns its keep at 8:15 am, not where it wins the honeymoon slideshow.

Masséna / Carré d’Or: the all-round compromise

Central, close to the sea, easy for Old Nice and strong for evenings. For many first visits, this is the least complicated place to stay. You trade a little railway convenience for a more immediately holiday-shaped setting.

Old Nice / Garibaldi: atmosphere first

Best if you want dinner, narrow streets and the old city outside the door. The catch is obvious but frequently ignored until the suitcase arrives: noise, pedestrian lanes and less direct access to the station. Charm has never carried luggage.

Port: east-side character and a different rhythm

A strong choice if you like the Port, Garibaldi and the eastern side of the city, or if Villefranche and Cap-Ferrat matter more than repeated western trips. It feels less “central Nice postcard” and more like a neighbourhood with a harbour attached. We mean that as a compliment.

When Nice is not the right base

Your priority is sand and hotel-beach life. Cannes or Antibes may fit better. Nice has the city and the transport; the pebbles remain magnificently unmoved by your preferences.

Your trip is mostly western Riviera. If Cannes, Antibes and the Cap dominate, Antibes can reduce backtracking and give you a softer base.

You want the hotel to be the holiday. Cap-Ferrat, Cap d’Antibes, Cannes or Monaco have stronger arguments for a stay built around the property itself.

You want Saint-Tropez and inland villages every other day. Then the no-car Nice logic starts to bend. A car or a second base may finally earn its keep.

THE DECISION

Nice is not “the best place on the Riviera”. It is the best first base for the widest range of first trips. Different claim. Much more useful.

A first-trip order that usually works

Arrival day: stay in Nice. First full day: learn Nice before escaping it. Then add the coast in different directions rather than repeating the same mood — Villefranche/Cap-Ferrat for beauty, Monaco/Menton for the east, Antibes for the west. Keep one half-day loose. Weather, energy and lunch have a democratic right to alter the plan.

From the airport

Tram line 2 serves both terminals, Jean Médecin and Port Lympia. Lignes d’Azur currently states roughly one airport tram every eight minutes on weekdays. The dedicated Aéro return ticket is currently listed at €10. Check before travelling, because public transport occasionally develops new opinions.

What we would not do

Spend the entire trip on the Promenade des Anglais. Nearly 7 km, very beautiful, still not the whole city.

Do Nice, Èze, Monaco and Menton in one day. You will return with excellent station photography and very little memory of the places between the stations.

Arrive at Cours Saleya at noon in August and complain that Nice is touristy. You and half of Europe had exactly the same idea. Nobody set a trap for you. You are the traffic.
