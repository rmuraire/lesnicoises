RIVIERA GUIDE • SAINT-TROPEZ

Saint-Tropez from Nice: worth the expedition?

Yes, if Saint-Tropez is one of the reasons you came. No, if you are adding it because a Riviera checklist has started issuing orders.

Full dayFrom Nice, this is an excursion, not a casual detour.
Boat wins in seasonThe cleanest no-car version when the direct service is running.
Do not add Pampelonne by reflexVillage plus beach can become two different transport problems.

The decision first

Saint-Tropez is not on the railway. That single fact changes the day. From Nice, the question is not whether Saint-Tropez is famous enough to visit; it is whether you want it enough to spend a serious slice of the day getting there and back.

THE MAMETAS VERDICT

For a first five-day Riviera trip, Saint-Tropez is optional. Choose it over another eastern or western excursion, not on top of one. If you have seven days, care about the place and can use the direct boat, the argument becomes much stronger.

From Nice without a car: the clean version

Seasonal direct boat

For 2026, Trans Côte d’Azur runs a direct Nice–Saint-Tropez day cruise from 12 May to 20 October on selected days, with the densest schedule in July and August. The published timetable leaves Nice at 9:00, reaches Saint-Tropez at 11:30, gives you until 16:30 in the village and returns to Nice at 19:00. Reservation is required. Check the current operating day before building the trip around it.

This is the no-car option we prefer from Nice because the transport itself becomes part of the day and you avoid stitching together rail and road connections.

Train + regional bus

The more flexible public-transport fallback is train to Saint-Raphaël, then ZOU! bus 876 via Sainte-Maxime to Saint-Tropez. It works, but it is a multi-leg journey and deserves timetable discipline. Do not discover the return connection while ordering the second coffee.

What deserves your time

The old port + La Ponche. Start with the village itself. Saint-Tropez existed before beach clubs discovered typography.

Place des Lices. A useful pause and a better sense of the town than sprinting between branded windows.

Citadel or Musée de l’Annonciade. Choose one cultural anchor if you are on a day trip. Both are defensible; completionism is not.

Pampelonne only if the beach is the point. Pampelonne is outside the village and changes the logistics. If your fantasy is specifically Pampelonne, plan the transport around it. If your fantasy is Saint-Tropez the village, stay in town.

When we would skip it

You only have three days on the Riviera.

You already plan Monaco/Menton, Villefranche/Cap-Ferrat and Antibes in a five-day trip and do not want the holiday to become a transport demonstration.

The direct boat is not running and Saint-Tropez is a mild curiosity rather than a priority.
