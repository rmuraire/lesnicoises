Choose the base before the hotel

Nice or Cannes: where should you stay?

They are thirty-odd kilometres apart and produce surprisingly different holidays. The right answer depends less on which one photographs better and more on what you want to do after breakfast.

Nice and Cannes are not interchangeable Riviera bases. Nice behaves like a city with a beach attached. Cannes behaves more like a polished seaside stay with a city supporting it. Both can work beautifully. They simply optimise different things.

The Mametas answer

For a first Riviera trip of three to seven days, especially without a car, choose Nice unless you have a specific Cannes reason: sandy beaches, a compact hotel-and-seafront stay, or a strong preference for the western side of the coast.

Transport: Nice is the easier machine

If this is your first Riviera trip, transport is where Nice earns its keep. The airport sits on the western edge of the city, the tram takes you into the centre and Nice-Ville puts the coastal railway within easy reach. That makes Villefranche, Monaco, Menton, Antibes and Cannes realistic day trips without changing hotels.

Cannes is also on the coastal rail line and works perfectly well without a car. The difference is geometry. It is a stronger base for the western side of the Riviera, while eastern days towards Monaco and Menton become longer. If you intend to spend most of the week moving around, that accumulated friction matters.

Mametas rule

Do not choose the prettiest hotel before choosing the most useful base.

A rooftop pool is delightful. It remains surprisingly poor at getting you to Menton before lunch.

Beaches: Cannes wins if sand matters

This is the cleanest Cannes victory. Central Cannes has sandy beaches, including public stretches as well as the private beach-club world associated with the Croisette. Nice has its famous pebble shoreline. The water can be magnificent; the entry and exit are not designed for graceful people carrying a tote bag.

If your ideal day is hotel, sand, swim, lunch and very little transport, Cannes becomes substantially more persuasive. If the beach is one element among markets, museums, neighbourhoods and day trips, Nice's pebbles are easier to forgive.

Evenings: city life versus compact polish

Nice offers more versions of an evening. Old Nice, the Port, the central grid and other neighbourhoods give you a broader restaurant and bar landscape, and the city continues to feel inhabited once you leave the seafront.

Cannes is more compact. That can be a feature rather than a weakness: hotel, beach, Croisette, restaurants and Le Suquet can sit within a very manageable radius. For a short two- or three-night stay, there is something efficient about not turning dinner into another logistics problem.

Hotels: choose the role you want the hotel to play

Cannes has an unusually concentrated palace-hotel culture. Martinez, Carlton and Majestic are not merely places to sleep; they are part of the Cannes proposition. If that performance is what you came for, pretending otherwise is unnecessary.

Nice gives you a broader set of hotel personalities and locations, although our current Mametas shortlist is still stronger at the expensive end than at sensible mid-range prices. We are fixing that rather than filling the gap with recommendations we cannot defend.

Day trips: the coast pulls in different directions

From Nice

Villefranche and Cap-Ferrat, Èze, Monaco and Menton are the obvious eastern strengths. Antibes and Cannes remain straightforward western trips by train. That centrality is why Nice is so difficult to beat for a first visit.

From Cannes

Antibes is close, the Lérins Islands sit directly offshore and the western coastline becomes easier to prioritise. If the holiday is deliberately Cannes-centred rather than a Riviera survey course, this is a perfectly coherent strategy.

Saint-Tropez is not a casual add-on from either city. Seasonal boats, road traffic and connections deserve their own plan. Proximity on a map is not a transport timetable.

Value: neither city has signed a modesty agreement

Do not assume Nice is automatically cheap or Cannes automatically absurd. Both can become expensive in peak season and around major events. The better question is what you are paying for.

In Cannes, paying more for a hotel can make sense if the beach, pool and hotel itself are central to the trip. In Nice, we would more often spend for location and logistics, then use the city and the railway rather than treating the hotel as the destination.

Our verdict for a first trip

Choose Nice for three to seven days. It gives you more city, more contrast and a more forgiving transport network. You can still visit Cannes for the day and decide whether the Croisette deserves a future hotel bill.

Choose Cannes when you can state the reason clearly. You want sand. You want a compact seaside stay. You want the palace-hotel experience. You are concentrating on the western Riviera. Those are real reasons. “It looked glamorous” is technically a reason too; it is simply a more expensive one.

In one sentence

Nice is the better Riviera base. Cannes can be the better Riviera holiday.
