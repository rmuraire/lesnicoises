Gay Nice: where the useful bits actually are

Nice is a real city with a visible LGBTQ+ community, an official gay-friendly programme and enough ordinary Riviera life that you never have to choose between the two.

34 Nice Rainbow staysThe official 2026 accommodation directory currently lists 34 labelled places.

Check the live calendarNice updates its LGBTQIA+ events page regularly. Static “best bars” lists age badly.

Nice does not have one sealed-off gay district, and that is part of the appeal. LGBTQ+ venues are mixed into the centre, Old Nice and the Port, while the rest of the city works exactly as a Riviera base should. The useful distinction is this: Nice Rainbow is an inclusion and welcome label, not a promise that every guest in the room is gay.

Mametas rule: use the label when it helps you choose a hotel, restaurant or venue with an explicit welcome standard. Use the rest of the city normally. You came to Nice, not to an airport lounge with a rainbow sticker.

Where to go out: four addresses that actually do different jobs

We are deliberately not giving you fifteen bars. These four cover very different moments of the night, and all are currently referenced by Nice Côte d’Azur’s LGBTQ+ material.

Ramdam Bar

3 rue Lascaris · Port

Start here if you want a bar rather than a club: drinks, music, exhibitions and an explicitly LGBTQIA+ identity.

Best used earlier in the evening or as the social part of the night before deciding whether you actually want to dance until 5am.

Le Glam

6 rue Eugène Emanuel · Centre

The classic weekend-club answer: DJs, themed nights, shows and performances.

The tourism office currently lists Friday, Saturday and Sunday nights, midnight–5am. Check the programme before going.

Club Le 6

6 rue Raoul Bosio · Old Nice

More bar-meets-club than giant nightclub: DJs, live music, karaoke and themed evenings.

Current official hours run Tuesday–Saturday, 10pm–5am, with broader summer opening.

Le Klubber

14 rue Benoît Bunico · Old Nice

The after-party answer. Not where we would begin the evening; very much where you may end it.

Official tourism information describes weekend after-hours roughly from 4:30/5am into late morning. Dawn is now a scheduling choice.

FIRST-NIGHT LOGIC

Port or Garibaldi for an early drink, dinner nearby, then choose Le Glam or Le 6 if the night still has ambitions. Klubber only if breakfast has become a theoretical concept.

Where to eat without turning dinner into “gay tourism”

Nice Rainbow also labels mainstream restaurants. That matters if you value an explicit welcome standard; it does not mean these are “gay restaurants”. Three useful examples:

Côté Marais

4 rue du Pontin · Old Nice

Small-format French dinner: the official listing gives just 22 indoor covers, plus a small terrace.

Nice Rainbow · French restaurant · evening service on listed opening days.

Davisto

18 rue Saint-Philippe

Italian rather than Niçois: fresh, seasonal, house-made cooking and a useful option west of the centre.

Nice Rainbow · Italian / Mediterranean · lunch and dinner on most listed opening days.

Sentimi

2–4 place Garibaldi

The easiest pairing with a Port/Garibaldi evening: large terrace, Italian-Mediterranean menu and no detour required.

Nice Rainbow · open daily in the current tourism listing.

Where to swim

Castel Plage, at the eastern end of the Promenade beneath Castle Hill, is currently listed with Nice Rainbow. It is the straightforward option if you want a private beach with an explicit gay-friendly signal.

But for the prettiest swimming day, we would still leave the city centre and go towards Villefranche-sur-Mer or Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat. There is no requirement for every good swim to be a “gay beach”. See the Mametas beach guide →

What is on while you are there

This is the part we refuse to freeze into a static article. Nice Côte d’Azur maintains a dedicated LGBTQIA+ calendar and says it is refreshed regularly. The wider annual ecosystem includes events such as Pink Parade, IN&OUT and Dolly Party, while smaller parties, sports events, cabaret and community nights change week by week.

The local resource worth knowing

Centre LGBTQIA+ Côte d’Azur

Rue Cathy Richeux · Nice

Not a tourist attraction: a real community and resource centre bringing together 18 LGBTQIA+ associations.

It runs social events and a community bar, as well as support and sexual-health services. Useful if you want something more local than nightlife.

A simple 48-hour version

Day one: Old Nice, Castle Hill, the Port and a swim. Early evening around Port/Garibaldi; use Ramdam if you want a specifically LGBTQ+ starting point, then dinner. Check the live programme before choosing a club.

Day two: Nice as a city first — culture, market, sea — then decide whether you want another LGBTQ+ night at all. If not, excellent. That is the point of choosing a city with a scene rather than a scene pretending to be a city.

THE DECISION

Nice works because the LGBTQ+ layer is visible when you want it and easy to ignore when you do not. Base yourself well, use a few current addresses, then get on with the Riviera.
