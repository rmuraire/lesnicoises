HONEYMOON · FRENCH RIVIERA

Romantic is easy. Logistics are the part people forget.

The Riviera can do honeymoon very well. The trick is not booking the prettiest hotel on the map and then spending the week moving luggage between postcards.

MAMETAS SAYS

For 4–5 nights, choose one base. For 7 nights, add a second only if it changes the trip: a resort stay on Cap-Ferrat, sand and old-town life in Antibes, or a deliberate Saint-Tropez finish. Changing hotels just to collect place names is not romantic. It is admin with better linen.

The 5-night version we would actually book

DAY 1

Arrive, do almost nothing

Check in, walk to the sea, have a proper dinner close to the hotel. Honeymoon rule number one: the first evening does not need a transfer, a timed ticket and three villages.

DAY 2

Nice properly, not as an airport with a beach

Old Nice, Castle Hill, lunch, sea. Keep the evening free for the dinner you actually cared enough to reserve.

DAY 3

Villefranche + Cap-Ferrat

Take the short train to Villefranche, then choose one beautiful thing rather than five: the coast, Villa Ephrussi, or a long lunch. Villa Ephrussi sits above the peninsula with nine themed gardens.

DAY 4

Èze, early or late

The view earns its reputation; the crowds do too. The Exotic Garden sits at 429 m above the sea, and its official average individual visit is around an hour. Go early, or accept that your romantic medieval village has other people in it.

DAY 5

Your choice: Antibes or stay put

If sand and old-town atmosphere matter, Antibes is an easy train day. If you are finally relaxed, do not ruin it because an itinerary says you still owe the Riviera another town.

Four honeymoon mistakes we would avoid

  • Three hotels in five nights. You married each other, not your luggage.
  • A sea-view hotel with terrible access for the trip you planned. Check station, slopes and evening walk.
  • Driving everywhere because it sounds glamorous. Nice–Villefranche–Monaco–Antibes–Cannes works remarkably well by train.
  • Scheduling romance. One empty afternoon is not wasted inventory.
