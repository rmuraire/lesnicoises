LIVING HERE · RETIREMENT

Do not choose your retirement town from the balcony view.

Choose it from the Tuesday in February when you need groceries, a doctor, a train and somewhere pleasant to walk. The balcony can still be excellent.

MAMETAS SAYS

Rent before you buy, and test winter as seriously as summer. The Riviera is easy to fall for in June. Retirement is the part where you still need the place to work in November, February and during a heatwave.

First: the residence rule depends on your passport

EU/EEA/Swiss citizens and non-EU nationals do not have the same process. France-Visas states that people who need a long-stay visa and want to stay in France privately for more than three months without working can apply for a long-stay “visitor” visa; applicants must show resources, accommodation and medical cover and commit not to work. Use the official visa assistant for your nationality and situation.

Important: France also has a residence card labelled “retired”, but it is a specific status for certain former French residents receiving a French basic pension. It is not the generic visa for anyone who happens to be retired.

Car-free retirement is possible. Address-level car-free retirement is the real test.

The coastal railway makes Nice, Antibes, Cannes, Monaco and Menton unusually connected. Nice also has a strong tram network. But “the town has a station” is not enough: walk the route from the specific apartment to the station, supermarket and pharmacy. Ten minutes on a map can include a hill that becomes your least favourite feature of Provence.

Healthcare: do not assume eligibility from geography

Healthcare access and who pays for it depend on residence status, prior insurance rights and your individual situation. Non-EU visitor-visa applicants are explicitly asked to show medical cover. Before moving, establish which system will cover you, how prescriptions transfer, and which doctors you would realistically use. The Riviera has substantial healthcare provision; entitlement is a separate question.

The property checklist changes after 60

  • Lift: not “there is one somewhere”, but whether it reaches your floor without half a staircase.
  • Heat and air conditioning: test both winter comfort and summer heat.
  • Noise: visit at night and during peak season.
  • Outdoor space: lovely; maintenance and stairs still count.
  • Station / shops / doctor: measure the walk yourself.
  • Year-round building: a residence full of holiday lets behaves differently from a residential building.

The six-week experiment

If retirement on the Riviera is serious, we would rather spend money on a long rental than on a property-scouting weekend. Stay three weeks in winter and three in shoulder season. Live normally. Cook. Take the train. Deal with paperwork. See friends. Be bored once. If you still like the town when nothing special is happening, that is useful evidence.
