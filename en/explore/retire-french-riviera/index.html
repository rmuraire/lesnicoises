LIVING HERE · RETIREMENT

Retiring here is not an extended holiday.

Which is good news. The Riviera is strongest when you judge it by Tuesday morning, not the hotel balcony: doctors, trains, markets, stairs, winter light and people you might actually know.

Mametas CheckedSources and practical details checked · 30 September 2026
MAMETAS SAYS

Nice for medical depth and no-car life. Antibes for balance. Menton for a smaller, slower daily radius. The retirement decision is not which promenade looks best in August; it is which Tuesday in January you want to live.

Three credible bases

MOST COMPLETE

Nice

Best for healthcare depth, airport access, culture, public transport and year-round choice. You can live without a car more easily than almost anywhere else on the Riviera.

The catch: it is a real city: noise, crowds and price variation by neighbourhood matter.

BALANCED

Antibes

Strong if you want a smaller city, sea, station, Old Town life and access to Sophia / Cannes / Nice.

The catch: seasonal housing pressure and very different neighbourhood lifestyles.

SLOWER

Menton

Best for a quieter rhythm, Italian edge and gentler scale while keeping a train connection to Monaco and Nice.

The catch: airport access and some specialist needs require more planning.

Healthcare and residency are not side notes

EU/EEA/Swiss retirees: Service-Public says you can live in France beyond three months without a compulsory residence card, but as an inactive/retired resident you need sufficient resources and health insurance. A residence card is optional but can simplify proof of status.

Non-EU retirees: France-Visas says a private stay of more than three months without working normally requires a long-stay “visitor” visa for people subject to the visa requirement, with evidence of resources, accommodation and medical coverage. Nationality-specific rules apply.

Healthcare: PUMa covers people who work or reside in France on a stable and regular basis, but retirees arriving from another European system may instead use an S1 transfer route. Do not assume that buying property creates health-cover rights.

Important: immigration, healthcare and taxation depend on nationality, pension history and tax residence. Use the official sources below for your own situation rather than treating any general guide as legal or tax advice.

Tax deserves its own appointment

French tax residence depends on facts such as where your household and main stay are, and international tax treaties can change how pensions are taxed. Impots.gouv.fr states that foreign pensions received by French tax residents may need to be declared in France, while treaty rules address double taxation. Resolve this before a property purchase, not after.

Car or no car?

If you live in central Nice, Antibes or Menton, no-car life is realistic. If your dream is a villa above the coast, the dream probably includes driving. Test gradients as seriously as square metres.

Rent before buying

A six- or twelve-month rental teaches you more than ten property weekends: winter sun, summer noise, doctor access, building quality, neighbour turnover and whether the “quiet” street still feels useful after dark.

The boring questions that decide quality of life

  • Can you walk to food, pharmacy and a train or tram?
  • Does the building have a lift?
  • How hot is the flat in August and how cold in January?
  • Where is your GP / specialist care likely to come from?
  • Who will you actually see on a Wednesday?

Why Antibes deserves a serious look

INSEE reports that 27.7% of Antibes residents were 65 or older in 2023. It is not a retirement resort, but older residents are a substantial part of the real year-round city.

What “€26/m²” means in real life

For a rough same-size comparison, a 50 m² two-room apartment at the current median-per-m² estimate works out to about €1,300/month in Nice, €1,200 in Antibes and €1,300 in Menton, before charges. This is deliberately mechanical: neighbourhood, condition, sea view, furnished status and season can move a real listing far away from the median.

The health infrastructure is not equivalent

Nice: CHU Pasteur 2 alone lists 640 beds and 118 day places, with emergency, cardiology, neurology, nephrology, trauma and other specialties. Antibes: the public hospital runs general and psychiatric emergencies 24/7. Menton: La Palmosa runs 24/7 emergency care and is the local reference hospital for the Riviera Française territory, but it is a much smaller hospital than Nice’s CHU network.

The practical implication: Menton’s slower scale is real, but so is the greater likelihood of travelling west for some specialist care. That does not make it a bad retirement base; it changes what “easy” means.

Residency, healthcare and tax: keep the three files separate

EU/EEA/Swiss residence rights, non-EU visitor visas, French health cover (including possible S1 routes for some European pensioners) and tax residence are different systems. Property ownership does not solve any of them automatically. Use the official links below and professional advice for your own case.

2026 WATCH

What changed for foreign retirees this year?

The “free French healthcare” shortcut is no longer a safe assumption.

The 2026 Social Security Financing Act created a financial contribution for certain PUMa beneficiaries who live in France stably, do not work and are not liable for CSG, CRDS or a health-insurance contribution because of an international convention. As of 1 October 2026, Service-Public still says the decree setting the amount is pending.

This is particularly relevant to some American retirees. A June 2026 government response to Parliament explicitly discusses U.S. pensioners who can access PUMa through residence while their pension is taxed under the U.S.–France treaty, and says the new contribution was created to make some such residents contribute to healthcare. People covered through another state under European coordination or a bilateral social-security arrangement can be treated differently.

Tax treaty does not mean “no French tax return”

If you become French tax resident, foreign pensions still have French reporting obligations. Impots.gouv.fr’s 2026 guidance requires foreign pensions to be reported on the French return and, depending on treaty treatment, on form 2047 and the relevant pension boxes. The treaty decides where and how the income is ultimately taxed; it does not remove the filing step.

Mametas 2026 rule: if your retirement plan depends on a zero-euro healthcare contribution or on “my U.S. pension is not taxed in France, so I do not file it”, stop and get current professional advice before signing a lease or buying.

Retirement FAQ

Can Americans retire in France?

Potentially, but a non-EU retiree normally needs the appropriate long-stay visitor route and must meet resource, accommodation and medical-cover requirements. Nationality-specific rules apply.

Which Riviera city is easiest without a car?

Nice. It has the deepest tram/bus network, direct airport tram and the largest hospital infrastructure. Antibes and Menton work without a car if you choose a central, walkable address near the station.

How much is rent?

August 2026 market estimates put median apartment rent around €28/m² in Nice, €25/m² in Antibes and €27/m² in Menton. Two-room estimates were about €26, €24 and €26/m² respectively.

Which has the oldest population?

Using INSEE 2023 age bands, residents aged 65+ were about 23.4% in Nice, 27.7% in Antibes and 25.2% in Menton.

What hospital serves each city?

Nice has CHU Pasteur 2 and other CHU sites; Antibes has Centre Hospitalier d’Antibes with 24/7 general emergencies; Menton has La Palmosa with 24/7 emergency care.

Is Menton too quiet in winter?

It is the smallest and slowest of the three, but it is a real year-round town with a hospital and frequent trains to Nice. Whether that feels calm or too quiet is precisely why a winter rental is useful before buying.

What changed for some American retirees in France in 2026?

The 2026 Social Security Financing Act created a financial contribution for certain PUMa beneficiaries who live in France stably, do not work and are not liable for CSG, CRDS or a health-insurance contribution because of an international convention. As of 1 October 2026, the implementing decree setting the amount is still pending.
