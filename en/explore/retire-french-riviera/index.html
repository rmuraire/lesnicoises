LIVING HERE · RETIREMENT

Retiring here is not an extended holiday.

Which is good news. The Riviera is strongest when you judge it by Tuesday morning, not the hotel balcony: doctors, trains, markets, stairs, winter light and people you might actually know.

Mametas CheckedSources and practical details checked · 30 September 2026
MAMETAS SAYS

Do not choose your retirement town from August. Spend time here in winter, do errands, use public transport and see whether the daily version still works.

Three credible bases

MOST COMPLETE

Nice

Best for healthcare depth, airport access, culture, public transport and year-round choice. You can live without a car more easily than almost anywhere else on the Riviera.

The catch: it is a real city: noise, crowds and price variation by neighbourhood matter.

BALANCED

Antibes

Strong if you want a smaller city, sea, station, Old Town life and access to Sophia / Cannes / Nice.

The catch: seasonal housing pressure and very different neighbourhood lifestyles.

SLOWER

Menton

Best for a quieter rhythm, Italian edge and gentler scale while keeping a train connection to Monaco and Nice.

The catch: airport access and some specialist needs require more planning.

Healthcare and residency are not side notes

EU/EEA/Swiss retirees: Service-Public says you can live in France beyond three months without a compulsory residence card, but as an inactive/retired resident you need sufficient resources and health insurance. A residence card is optional but can simplify proof of status.

Non-EU retirees: France-Visas says a private stay of more than three months without working normally requires a long-stay “visitor” visa for people subject to the visa requirement, with evidence of resources, accommodation and medical coverage. Nationality-specific rules apply.

Healthcare: PUMa covers people who work or reside in France on a stable and regular basis, but retirees arriving from another European system may instead use an S1 transfer route. Do not assume that buying property creates health-cover rights.

Important: immigration, healthcare and taxation depend on nationality, pension history and tax residence. Use the official sources below for your own situation rather than treating any general guide as legal or tax advice.

Tax deserves its own appointment

French tax residence depends on facts such as where your household and main stay are, and international tax treaties can change how pensions are taxed. Impots.gouv.fr states that foreign pensions received by French tax residents may need to be declared in France, while treaty rules address double taxation. Resolve this before a property purchase, not after.

Car or no car?

If you live in central Nice, Antibes or Menton, no-car life is realistic. If your dream is a villa above the coast, the dream probably includes driving. Test gradients as seriously as square metres.

Rent before buying

A six- or twelve-month rental teaches you more than ten property weekends: winter sun, summer noise, doctor access, building quality, neighbour turnover and whether the “quiet” street still feels useful after dark.

The boring questions that decide quality of life

  • Can you walk to food, pharmacy and a train or tram?
  • Does the building have a lift?
  • How hot is the flat in August and how cold in January?
  • Where is your GP / specialist care likely to come from?
  • Who will you actually see on a Wednesday?

Why Antibes deserves a serious look

INSEE reports that 27.7% of Antibes residents were 65 or older in 2023. It is not a retirement resort, but older residents are a substantial part of the real year-round city.
