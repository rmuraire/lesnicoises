LIVING HERE · ANTIBES

Antibes is international enough to land softly. It is still France.

That combination is exactly why many people like it. Tech, yachts, English-speaking circles and international schools exist around you; French administration, summer crowds and housing pressure do not disappear because the sea is blue.

MAMETAS SAYS

Antibes works best for people who want a real town, not an expat compound. You can live near the market and sea, commute into an international employment ecosystem, and still need French when the boiler breaks.

Why there is such an international community here

The answer is not only tourism. Sophia Antipolis sits inland from Antibes and describes itself as home to more than 160 nationalities, with almost a third of employees coming from international backgrounds. The area also has a dense concentration of international schools. Add Port Vauban and the yachting economy, and Antibes has several different international populations overlapping in the same town.

Where to live depends on what you are solving

OLD TOWN

Vieil Antibes

Best for walking, restaurants, market and feeling that you actually moved to the Mediterranean. The catch: stairs, summer pressure, deliveries and parking can become daily-life topics.

TRAIN + BEACH

Central Antibes / Ponteil

Useful compromise between station, old town and sea. Strong if you do not want every errand to require a car.

SAND + EVENINGS

Juan-les-Pins

More resort rhythm, sandy beaches and its own station. Livelier in season, quieter outside it.

SPACE

Cap / residential areas

More calm and greenery; more reason to think seriously about a car or bike for daily life.

The Sophia Antipolis commute is the question, not an afterthought

If work is in Sophia, choose your exact office before choosing your apartment. The technopole is spread out and “Antibes to Sophia” is not one commute. Bus connections exist, cycling is possible on some routes, and driving remains common. A beautiful flat that adds 45 frustrating minutes twice a day becomes less beautiful surprisingly quickly.

For coast travel, Antibes is much easier: the official tourism office notes that Antibes, Juan-les-Pins and Biot are served by regional trains, while Nice Airport is about 20 km away.

Do you need French?

You can build an English-speaking social life remarkably fast around tech, yachting and international families. That is not the same thing as building a life without French. Landlords, tradespeople, schools, healthcare, taxes and local associations become easier as your French improves. The international bubble is useful. It is a poor long-term substitute for the place you moved to.

Visa and work rights: separate the lifestyle decision from the legal one

EU/EEA/Swiss citizens and non-EU nationals do not follow the same residence rules. For non-EU nationals, the correct route depends on whether you are working, joining family, studying or arriving under another status. Use the official France-Visas assistant before making relocation plans around a property viewing.

Our “try before you move” test

  • Rent for at least a few weeks outside July–August.
  • Do your actual commute at the hour you would actually commute.
  • Buy groceries, use the station, make one medical appointment, work from home, find parking if you plan to drive.
  • Spend one rainy week here. A place that only works in beach weather is a holiday.
Who usually loves Antibes? People who want the Riviera but still want a town: walkable centre, beach, station, year-round life, and access to a serious international employment zone. Who may prefer elsewhere? People whose work and social life are entirely in central Nice, or who want complete resort quiet.
