LIVING HERE · ANTIBES

Antibes is not just where expats end up. There are reasons.

Sea, trains, Sophia Antipolis, yachting and a year-round town explain the international pull. The useful question is whether its daily compromises fit your version of Riviera life.

MAMETAS SAYS

Choose Antibes only if your work and daily geography match it. Old Antibes is a walkable railway town; Sophia is an inland employment zone; Juan-les-Pins is a seasonal beach district. “Antibes” is not one lifestyle.

Why there are so many internationals around Antibes

Sophia Antipolis brings global technology and research employers; Port Vauban and the yachting economy bring another international ecosystem; tourism adds seasonal multilingual work. The result is not an “expat bubble” in one neighbourhood so much as several overlapping international networks. The official Antibes tourism office describes Port Vauban as a 1,501-berth marina supporting shipyards, crew agencies and yachting services, and explicitly notes the cosmopolitan feel around the old town.

Where daily life feels different

VIEIL ANTIBES

Walkable, social, charming

Restaurants, market, station within reach, sea nearby. Best if you want daily life to happen on foot.

The catch: small flats, noise, stairs and summer pressure can be very real.

JUAN-LES-PINS

Beach rhythm + station

More seasonal, more resort-like, but practical if sand and rail access matter.

The catch: winter and summer do not feel like the same town.

AROUND SOPHIA / VALBONNE / BIOT

Work-first geography

Stronger if office access, schools and space matter more than walking to dinner.

The catch: a car becomes much more useful.

The transport reality

Antibes station is excellent for the coastal axis: Nice, Cannes and Monaco are straightforward by TER. Sophia itself is inland. The CASA says Line B now links central Antibes to Sophia roughly every 12–15 minutes and is free with the Pass Navette; the wider Envibus network covers 24 communes. That is meaningful public transport, but your exact employer and home address still decide whether the commute is elegant or annoying. Test the real route at rush hour before signing a lease.

Housing: the Riviera tax is not only price

High secondary-home share means the market is influenced by seasonal and furnished stock. Ask early whether a rental is genuinely year-round, what is included, whether the building has a lift, and how summer noise changes the street.

French helps more than expat groups admit

You can build a social life in English around Sophia and yachting, but administration, healthcare, tradespeople, schools and deeper local integration get dramatically easier with working French.

Admin: your route depends on why you are moving

Do not copy another expat’s paperwork checklist. EU/EEA/Swiss free-movement rules, a French employment contract, an intra-group transfer, self-employment and a non-working move do not use the same route. France-Visas is the official starting point for people who need a visa; employers may also need prior work authorisation depending on the case.

Mametas rule: solve status, work rights and health cover before choosing a removal company.

Before moving: do a boring test week

  • Stay in the exact neighbourhood you are considering.
  • Commute to the actual workplace at the actual time.
  • Do grocery shopping, pharmacy, gym and a normal Tuesday dinner.
  • Test August mentally even if you visit in March.
  • Do not choose housing from the balcony alone.
The move test: if you still like Antibes after doing normal errands, you probably like Antibes rather than the idea of Antibes.

Three Antibes lives, now with a price and commute reality

Old Antibes: pay for walkability and the station; best if your work is coastal or hybrid. Juan-les-Pins: sand and station, but the seasonal swing is stronger. Sophia / Valbonne / Biot: better if school and office dominate; the coast becomes your leisure geography rather than your doorstep.

Before signing: ask whether a furnished lease is genuinely year-round. The “September to June” rhythm exists on the Riviera because some owners reserve summer for short-term letting; do not assume “long-term” means twelve months until the contract says so.

Living in Antibes FAQ

How much is rent in Antibes?

Le Figaro’s August 2026 estimate put median apartment rent at €25/m² overall, €24/m² for 2-room apartments and €22/m² for 3-room apartments. Treat this as a market indicator, not a listing quote.

How long is Antibes to Monaco by train?

SNCF listed 57 direct trains a day on 2 October 2026, with an average journey of 55 minutes and the fastest around 50 minutes.

How long is Antibes to Nice Airport by train?

Direct TER services from Antibes reach Nice Saint-Augustin in roughly 16–27 minutes on current timetables; the airport tram connection is at Grand Arénas / Saint-Augustin.

Do I need a car if I work in Sophia Antipolis?

Not necessarily. CASA’s Line B links central Antibes to Sophia every 12–15 minutes and is free with the Pass Navette, but the exact office and home address still determine whether the commute is easy.

What are international school fees near Antibes?

Published 2026–27 tuition ranges checked include about €15,960–25,600 at Mougins British International School and €12,780–16,995 at ICS Côte d’Azur; ASEICA’s public international sections use a much lower differentiated fee scale.

Is Antibes an expat bubble?

No. Sophia tech, Port Vauban yachting, tourism and long-term residents overlap, but they are not one community and they do not live on the same geography.
